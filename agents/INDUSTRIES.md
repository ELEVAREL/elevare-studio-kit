# Elevare Studio — Industry Playbooks (what a top-tier site in THIS sector must do)

[SKILLS-MAP.md](SKILLS-MAP.md) routes enterprise-vertical skills (healthcare, supply-chain, energy). This file covers the sectors the studio actually sells into — local service, retail, hospitality, practice, media — where the difference between a pretty site and a profitable one is a handful of sector-specific decisions.

**How to use it:** `product-strategist` names the vertical in the brief and pastes that section into the plan. `ux-designer` builds the flow around the **money moment**. `conversion-copywriter` writes to the **trust proof**. `seo-specialist` ships the **schema**. `design-auditor` and `qa-verifier` check the **#1 failure** before ship. Pair with [ARCHETYPES.md](ARCHETYPES.md) — most of these are archetype **D (local trust/service)**, and the exceptions are marked.

**The house payment model** (applies to every vertical below, so the per-section "booking/payment" lines assume it) *(model settled 2026-08-30; see `BUSINESS-PLAN.md` §2)*: Elevare builds for little or nothing up front and **invoices a percentage of the money the client earns through the site Elevare built**. **Both rates apply to the same client, per transaction type: 10% on deposits/bookings, 5% on everything else.** A salon taking a $50 online deposit and selling a $40 product online pays $5 + $2 that month; a pure shop only ever hits 5%. It is NOT "10% businesses vs 5% businesses". **BILLED, NEVER SKIMMED** — the customer pays the client, the client receives **100% of their own revenue into their own account**, and Elevare reads the earnings from the database it already owns, computes the fee, and sends an **invoice the client pays like any supplier bill, monthly in arrears**. Elevare never sits between a business and its customers' money: there is no deduction, no fee-split, and no application fee. Billing starts at the client's **first sale** — no sales, no invoice. Only money flowing **through the site** counts; the $150 paid in the chair or across the counter is out of scope. **Booking and deposit verticals take a deposit from the customer** (which is also what reduces no-shows) and the deposit is a charge on the client's **own** processor account — so when a section below says "deposit", that is the client's own charge, not anything Elevare routes. Rates are flat and per-client, set by hand at onboarding; **a grandfathered rate is never changed without the client's sign-off.** **Elevare holds the domain and hosting; the client owns their payment-processor account, Google Business Profile, handles, and mailbox** — on a client's own Stripe account Elevare joins as a **Developer** (keys and webhooks, no money movement). Elevare's own invoicing rail is **Square** (Mercury/ACH for payment); Elevare has no Stripe platform account and never will. The fee is disclosed in the client agreement every time. Authority: `BUSINESS-PLAN.md` §2 and memory `elevare-operating-model`; wiring is `payments-engineer`'s.

**★ Universal credential gate — run it BEFORE pasting any section into a brief.** Every "required" and "trust proof" item below that names a **regulated claim** — licensed · insured · bonded · certified · registered · accredited · a license/registration number · years in business · a rating · before/after photos of "our work" · a warranty — is a claim this *particular* client may not be entitled to make. The sections describe an established operator; a new one has none of it, and the default then becomes a claims violation rather than a best practice. So ask, per client, before design: registered with the governing board/state, insured, own photographs of own completed jobs, real reviews, real tenure. **Every "no" deletes the corresponding required item and its schema field** (`AggregateRating` with no reviews is fabricated data, not markup). §4 carries the worked example — Silverback Fence, 2026-09-05, five prescribed items banned outright — but the class is not §4's: it lives in §2 (cosmetology), §3 (instructor certification), §5 (professional licensure, the strictest), §7 (bar/CPA/real-estate admission) and anywhere a number implies an authority granted it. The honest substitutes are availability, responsiveness, a written fixed price, and saying plainly that the operator is new. **A vertical default is a starting point, never a permission slip** — `product-strategist` runs this gate at intake, `conversion-copywriter` may not write past it, and any "no" that still needs a claim goes to `compliance-counsel`.

**Universal to every local vertical** (don't repeat it per section): NAP + today's hours in header and footer · tap-to-call and tap-for-directions as real controls · a trust signal within view of every primary CTA · one page per service and per service area · sub-2s load on a mid-range phone · real photography of the actual place and actual people, never stock · `LocalBusiness` JSON-LD with the most specific subtype available · Google Business Profile treated as the front door the site must match exactly (name, hours, categories, photos).

## 1. Restaurant / bar / café
**Money moment:** a reservation, an order, or a decision to drive over. Usually decided in under 30 seconds, on a phone, while hungry.
**Above the fold:** cuisine + neighborhood, tonight's hours with an honest Open/Closed, and the single action that fits the business (Reserve / Order online / Directions).
**Required:** the **menu as real HTML text, never a PDF or a photo of a chalkboard** — a PDF menu is the single most common and most costly mistake in this vertical (unsearchable, unreadable on a phone, invisible to AI answers). Plus hours (incl. holiday exceptions), parking, an events/specials surface the owner can actually update, and photos of *food as served*.
**Trust proof:** recent reviews naming a dish · press/award mentions · a face — the owner or chef, named.
**Schema:** `Restaurant` (+ `Menu`, `MenuItem`), `OpeningHoursSpecification` with exceptions, `hasMap`, `servesCuisine`, `acceptsReservations`.
**Booking/payment:** reservation platform embed or a direct booking form; online ordering via the existing POS if one exists — **never rebuild ordering that a POS already handles well**; check what they run before proposing anything.
**Compliance:** allergen and nutrition claims are legally loaded — publish only what the client confirms in writing. Alcohol content may need age-gating and can restrict ad placement.
**#1 failure:** a beautiful site whose menu is a PDF and whose hours are wrong. Fix those two and you've beaten most competitors before design starts.

## 2. Salon / barber / braiding / beauty
**Money moment:** booking a specific service with a specific person at a specific time.
**Above the fold:** the work itself (portfolio image, not a stock model), the service category, and Book.
**Required:** a **service menu with real prices or honest ranges** and honest duration; a per-stylist page where the business is personality-led; a portfolio/gallery that is genuinely the studio's own work; a deposit/cancellation policy stated plainly *before* booking, not after.
**Trust proof:** before/after galleries with consistent lighting · reviews naming the stylist · texture/hair-type specificity where the specialty demands it (a braiding salon that doesn't name what it specializes in loses the client who was searching for exactly that).
**Schema:** `HealthAndBeautyBusiness` / `BeautySalon` + `Service` + `Offer` with price, `aggregateRating` only if reviews are real and on-site.
**Booking/payment:** booking platform or in-house scheduler; **deposits materially reduce no-shows** — if the client takes them, disclose the amount and the refund window on the booking screen itself.
**Compliance:** consent and image rights for every client photo used. Price display must match what's charged.
**#1 failure:** "Call to book" as the only path. This vertical's customer books at 11pm.

## 3. Fitness / martial arts / studio
**Money moment:** a free trial, an intro offer, or a class booking — almost never a cold membership purchase.
**Above the fold:** who it's for (kids / beginners / competitors), where, and the low-commitment first step.
**Required:** a **live class schedule** (stale schedules kill trust faster than bad design), instructor bios with real credentials, pricing that doesn't hide behind "contact us", and a clear first-visit expectation — what to wear, what happens, how long.
**Trust proof:** student results and belt/competition milestones · length of time in the community · parent testimonials for kids' programs.
**Schema:** `SportsActivityLocation` / `ExerciseGym`, `Event` for classes and seminars, `Offer` for the intro.
**Booking/payment:** trial signup → nurture. Recurring membership billing via Stripe subscriptions; **never** build a public multi-user subscription without reading the studio's own no-subscription-for-multi-user-app rule first.
**Compliance:** liability waivers, and **parental consent + extra care on any surface handling minors' data or photos**.
**#1 failure:** hiding price. In this vertical price opacity reads as expensive, and the lead goes to the gym that published theirs.

## 4. Home services / trades / contractor
**Money moment:** a quote request or a phone call, often urgent (leak, outage, lockout).
**★ FIRST, ESTABLISH WHAT THE CLIENT CAN LAWFULLY AND TRUTHFULLY CLAIM. Most of this section assumes an established, registered, insured business with completed jobs and real reviews. A NEW operator has none of that, and the defaults below then become a claims violation rather than a best practice.** Ask before you design: is the client registered with the state board for this trade, do they carry insurance, do they have photographs of their OWN completed work, and do they have real reviews. Every "no" removes a prescribed element. On Silverback Fence (2026-09-05) the answer was no to all four: Rhode Island mandates contractor registration for work over $500, the owner had not filed, and FIVE of the requirements below were banned outright as a result. An agent routing that build against this section unmodified would have shipped an unlicensed contractor claiming to be licensed. **The vertical default is a starting point, not a permission slip** — and licensing, insurance and bonding are regulated claims, not marketing copy. When the answers are no, the honest substitutes are availability, responsiveness, a written fixed price, and being explicit that the operator is new; see the market-report pattern in `D:\clients\silverback-fence\docs\`.

**Above the fold:** service + area served + **Licensed & Insured** + phone as a tap target. Emergency availability if it exists — that is the whole offer for half this traffic.
**Required:** service-area pages by town, a real project gallery with before/after, the license number displayed, response-time expectation, and a quote form short enough to finish one-handed (name, phone, what's wrong, photo upload).
**Trust proof:** license/bond/insurance numbers · warranty terms · reviews naming the town · photos of the actual crew and trucks.
**Schema:** `HomeAndConstructionBusiness` (or the specific subtype: `Plumber`, `Electrician`, `RoofingContractor`), `areaServed`, `Service`, `AggregateRating`.
**Booking/payment:** quote-first. Deposits and progress payments via invoice links rather than checkout.
**Compliance:** license display requirements vary by state and trade — confirm before publishing a number. Estimate vs. binding-quote language matters legally; use the client's wording.
**#1 failure:** a slow, image-heavy site with a contact form and no phone number in the header. This customer will not fill out a form.

## 5. Health & wellness practice (dental · chiro · therapy · assisted living · vet)
**Money moment:** a new-patient appointment or, for senior living, a tour request from an adult child — a different reader than the resident.
**Above the fold:** condition or service, insurance/payment reality, and Book / Request a tour.
**Required:** provider bios with credentials and a photo, insurance and payment options stated plainly, new-patient forms, accessibility and parking info, and — for senior living — pricing transparency plus a path for the *family* researching at midnight.
**Trust proof:** credentials and affiliations · years in practice · reviews that are careful about health specifics · facility photography that is honest.
**Schema:** `MedicalBusiness` / `Dentist` / `VeterinaryCare`, `Physician` for providers, `MedicalSpecialty`, `acceptedPaymentMethod`, `isAcceptingNewPatients`.
**Booking/payment:** scheduling widget or request form. **Any intake form is a PHI boundary.**
**Compliance — the hardest in this list:** a form collecting health details may pull you into **HIPAA**; route through `healthcare-phi-compliance` and `compliance-counsel` *before* building it. No PHI in URLs, analytics, email bodies, or third-party embeds. No outcome claims the practice can't substantiate. Ad platforms restrict health targeting.
**#1 failure:** a generic intake form quietly collecting symptom detail into an un-vetted third-party inbox.

## 6. Retail / boutique / DTC apparel
**Archetype:** **B — editorial commerce**, not D. This is the Nike/Adidas lane; read that section first.
**Money moment:** add-to-cart, then surviving checkout.
**Required:** PDP depth (multi-angle real photography, size and fit truth, materials, care), search and filtering that actually work, shipping and returns answered before they're asked, sticky mobile add-to-cart, guest checkout, and a post-purchase experience that is designed rather than transactional.
**Trust proof:** reviews with customer photos · returns policy stated as a benefit · real inventory status, never fake urgency.
**Schema:** `Product` + `Offer` (price, availability, condition) + `AggregateRating` + `Review`, `BreadcrumbList`, `Organization`.
**Booking/payment:** Stripe Checkout or Payment Element per the studio's payments standards; wallets enabled; taxes and shipping correct before launch, not after.
**Compliance:** returns/refund policy, sales tax nexus, EU/UK accessibility duty on commerce, and **WCAG 2.2 AA on the checkout flow specifically** (focus not obscured, 24px targets, no redundant entry). Fake scarcity and fabricated discounts are both a legal and a brand risk — the studio does not ship either.
**#1 failure:** a beautiful homepage over a thin PDP. Design budget goes homepage → **PDP** → post-purchase, and the middle one is where the money is.

## 7. Professional services (legal · accounting · consulting · real estate)
**Money moment:** a consultation booking, and the decision is made on perceived competence and specificity.
**Above the fold:** who you serve, the specific problem you solve, and Book a consultation. Generic "full-service" positioning converts worse than a narrow claim.
**Required:** practice/service area pages, named people with real bios, case studies or representative matters, published fee structure or at least a fee *model*, and a genuinely useful content layer — this is the vertical where content marketing still wins.
**Trust proof:** credentials, bar/CPA/license numbers · named case outcomes where permitted · client logos with permission · publications and speaking.
**Schema:** `LegalService` / `AccountingService` / `RealEstateAgent`, `Person` for professionals, `Service`, `FAQPage` on real questions.
**Booking/payment:** consultation scheduling; retainer or invoice payments, not checkout.
**Compliance — heavily regulated advertising:** legal and financial marketing rules restrict outcome claims, testimonials, "specialist"/"expert" language, and comparative claims, and they vary by state and body. **Every claim goes through `compliance-counsel` and the client's own compliance review before publish.** Real estate carries Fair Housing constraints on both copy and imagery.
**#1 failure:** anonymous, credential-free copy that could belong to any firm. Specificity *is* the conversion mechanism here.

## 8. Local media / publisher
**Archetype:** **E — content/media.** See that section; this covers the business layer.
**Money moment:** two of them — the reader (subscription/newsletter) and the advertiser (rate card → booked ad).
**Required:** an article system the client can run without a developer, an **advertiser-facing surface with the real rate card and a way to book**, a newsletter capture that isn't a popup war, an archive/e-edition path, and designed ad slots with reserved space.
**Trust proof:** masthead and real bylines · circulation/readership numbers if honest · community history.
**Schema:** `NewsArticle` / `Article` with `datePublished` and `author`, `Organization` with `publishingPrinciples`, `BreadcrumbList`.
**Booking/payment:** ad booking and subscription billing via Stripe; ad sales usually still close by phone — the site's job is the rate card and the lead.
**Compliance:** clearly label sponsored content · corrections policy · photo rights for every submitted image.
**#1 failure:** building the reader experience and forgetting the advertiser is the one who pays. Ship the ad-sales surface in v1.

## 9. Events / venue / weddings
**Money moment:** a date inquiry or a tour request; the qualifier is always *is my date available*.
**Required:** real capacity and pricing ranges, a genuine gallery of events actually held there, floor plans, availability or at least a date-first inquiry form, vendor/catering rules, and packages that are comparable at a glance.
**Trust proof:** past-event galleries with permission · couple/organizer testimonials naming the date · preferred-vendor lists.
**Schema:** `EventVenue` / `Place` with `maximumAttendeeCapacity`, `Event` for public events, `Offer` for packages.
**Booking/payment:** date-first inquiry → tour → deposit. Deposits and payment schedules via invoice links, with the cancellation policy stated before payment.
**Compliance:** photo rights and privacy for every event image; contract and deposit-refund terms drafted with `compliance-counsel`.
**#1 failure:** a contact form that doesn't ask for the date, forcing an email round-trip before the venue can answer the only question that matters.

## 10. Nonprofit / community
**Money moment:** a donation or a volunteer signup — and recurring giving is worth far more than one-time.
**Required:** a concrete impact statement (what $X does), a donation flow with recurring as the *default-visible* option, a program/services surface, volunteer signup, financial transparency, and board/leadership.
**Trust proof:** outcome numbers you can source · financial transparency (990, ratings) · beneficiary stories with consent.
**Schema:** `NGO` / `Organization`, `DonateAction`, `Event` for fundraisers.
**Booking/payment:** Stripe recurring donations with a clear cancel path; receipting and tax-deductibility language handled correctly.
**Compliance:** state charitable-solicitation registration, correct tax-receipt language, and **consent for every beneficiary story and photo** — this vertical's most common ethical failure.
**#1 failure:** an emotive site with a donation flow buried three clicks deep and no recurring option.

## 11. Resale / secondhand / single-seller consignment (streetwear · luxury · vintage)
*(Added 2026-09-06 from PhyscoClosett — a one-person reseller of BAPE, Sp5der, Zara and Louis Vuitton, run entirely from a phone. Read §6 first; this is §6's inventory model turned inside out and it changes almost every field.)*
**Archetype:** **B — editorial commerce**, with the same restraint. The structural difference from §6 is that **every SKU is quantity 1**: there is no size run, no restock, and "sold" is permanent. That kills the standard commerce pattern (filter → variant picker → cart) and makes the unit of design the **individual piece**, not the product family.
**Money moment:** a DM-free purchase of one specific item, decided on whether the buyer believes this particular seller has this particular thing and will actually ship it.
**Above the fold:** the pieces themselves, priced, with condition visible — not a brand story. A resale buyer is scanning for one item and leaves in seconds if the inventory isn't immediately legible.
**Required:** one page per piece with **real measurements** (not just a size tag), the **named flaw** stated plainly, condition, and provenance in the seller's own words · a **lot number** per piece · ships-from city · sold pieces **stay visible, marked GONE** (they are the seller's track record and the strongest proof on the site) · shipping and returns answered before they're asked · a post-purchase order-status surface, because a resale buyer's default anxiety is *did a stranger just take my money*.
**Trust proof — the only defensible one is FREE, TRUE and UNCOPYABLE.** A single-seller resale site cannot out-inventory StockX/Grailed/Depop and must not try; the one claim the marketplaces structurally cannot make is **one named person picked every piece**. So: the named flaw (a seller who volunteers a defect is telling the truth about everything else), real measurements, the seller's face and voice, the GONE archive, and a real ships-from location. **The "100% Authentic" / Verified badge is BANNED** — it is an authenticity guarantee neither the client nor the studio is able to write for LV or BAPE, and offering it converts a legal position that is strong into one that is not.
**Schema:** `Product` + `Offer` with **`itemCondition`** (`UsedCondition` / `RefurbishedCondition`) and `availability` flipping to `SoldOut` rather than the page 404ing, `sku` = the lot number, `seller` as `Person`/`Organization`, `BreadcrumbList`. **No `AggregateRating` until real reviews exist.**
**Booking/payment:** the client's **own** processor account, always — on Square this means the client creates the application in **his own developer console and invites Elevare as a developer team member**; Elevare registers no application and owns no merchant relationship. Guest checkout, wallets, and a receipt that names the specific lot. Elevare's fee stays a monthly invoice in arrears, never a checkout skim (`BUSINESS-PLAN.md` §2).
**Compliance:** reselling **authentic** goods is legal (first-sale doctrine) — **the exposure is counterfeits, not resale**, and it is handled by design rather than by disclaimer. Brand names are **text only** (nominative fair use): no logos, no marks, no imitation trade dress, and the site's own name must not be adjacent to a famous mark (PhyscoClosett was renamed off "Playboyscloset" for exactly this). Then: returns/refund terms, privacy + terms **before** the first buyer name and address is stored, state sales-tax nexus and the 1099-K threshold — both the client's, with an accountant; flag, never advise. Route all of it to `compliance-counsel` **before launch**, and note that this vertical usually starts as a "demo" with none of these obligations and acquires all of them the day it takes a real order (PLAYBOOK §2, scope escalation).
**#1 failure:** faking marketplace scale — stock photography, invented review counts, an authenticity badge, a "collection" of three items dressed as a catalog. It reads as a scam to the exact audience that has been scammed before, and it discards the only advantage a single seller has.

## 12. User-generated content / community platform (social app · forum · reviews · photo uploads · any surface strangers post to)
*(Added 2026-09-07 from Whyza's moderation wave; **amended 2026-09-07 from its profile wave** with the identity/retention half — an Elevare-owned social app, the studio's first Trust & Safety build. **Read this the moment ANY project lets one person publish something another person will see**, not only when the product is "a social network": a review section, a photo upload, a comments thread, a public profile, a community board on a client site all import the obligations below, and they arrive the day the feature ships, not at scale.)*
**Archetype:** **E — content/media** for the public surface, **G — internal operator console** for the moderation queue. The queue is a real product with its own design bar, not an admin afterthought, and it is where the compliance evidence lives.
**Money moment:** two, and they are different problems. The **first contribution** — a platform with no contributors has no inventory, and the largest measurable drop is the moment a person is asked to make something public. Then the **second session**: nothing is monetizable about a person who posts once and never returns, and the strongest predictor of their coming back is whether they got to make the place *theirs* — a person who sets a photo, a bio and one link comes back; a person staring at a letter initial on a blank cover does not. Ship an identity surface in v1, not in v2.
**Above the fold:** other people's actual content. Not a value proposition, not an empty feed with a call to action — an empty state is the product being out of stock, so seed or curate rather than shipping a page that proves nobody is here.
**Required:** a **report control on every publishable object** (post, comment, profile, image, DM) reachable by signed-out people too · a **moderation queue a human works**, with the reason, the reporter count and the decision history on one screen · **notice to the author on every enforcement action, with an appeal path and a deadline** · a **strike/consequence ladder written down publicly** and matching what the code actually does · **published community guidelines** that name each prohibited category and what happens · an **age gate** consistent with the floor you set · a **retention/preservation story** for evidence, decided before the first report arrives. **Then the identity half, which is the one that gets deferred and shouldn't be:** a way to set an avatar and a cover (Whyza read `avatar_url` and `cover_url` in eight files and wrote them in none, so every profile on the product was a letter initial, permanently) · an **edit surface reachable in one tap from the profile** · **real counts** — followers, following AND posts · a **follow that follows**, with optimistic state and a server correction · a share/copy-link · and **empty states written for a person who has nothing yet**, since on this vertical that person is most of the population.
**Trust proof:** the moderation surface working in public — the appeal that gets answered, the guidelines that describe the real ladder, the notice that arrives when something is removed. **Never a claim of scale, an "AI-powered safety" badge, or a promise of a review time you cannot staff.** A platform is trusted for what it does about the worst thing on it, not the best. **And every number the product prints about a person must be real** — never a `—` placeholder rendered where a measurement belongs, never a preview-query length labelled as a total, never a contrast or reach figure the render path contradicts. On any platform whose pitch is *we show you why*, one invented stat costs the entire claim.
**Schema:** minimal and deliberate. `Organization` + `WebSite` on the marketing surface; `SocialMediaPosting`/`DiscussionForumPosting` **only** on genuinely public content. **The members' surface should be `noindex` and disallowed to AI crawlers, and those are two different mechanisms** — a `robots:{index:false}` on the route is not a `Disallow` in `robots.txt`, and neither implies the other (Whyza shipped one and a permanent comment claiming neither).
**Booking/payment:** usually none, and **keep it that way as long as you can** — the moment a UGC product touches money it re-opens PCI, refunds, money-transmission and creator-payout tax, on top of everything below. Creator "support" links that go to the creator's own third-party page keep the platform out of the flow entirely. If money must come in, it is the client's own processor, per the house model above.
**Compliance — the heaviest of any vertical here, and most of it fires from the FIRST USER with no size threshold.** The worked map is `D:\whyza\docs\LEGAL\COMPLIANCE-CHECKLIST.md` §4 (regime · applies? · why · gap) — copy that table's shape, not its answers. The load-bearing ones: **18 U.S.C. §2258A / REPORT Act — no small-provider exemption; a provider that obtains actual knowledge of apparent CSAM must report to NCMEC "as soon as reasonably possible" and preserve for one year.** ESP registration is a free portal (`esp.ncmec.org/registration`), credentials are issued by NCMEC after they verify the company, only the owner can complete it, and **turnaround is the cost — start it before launch, and never describe filing as working until it runs.** Then: **DMCA §512** (register an agent, publish the address, wire the strike flow), **Section 230** framing in the Terms, **COPPA** actual-knowledge duties plus an under-13 pipeline, state minors' duties if the age floor ever drops below 18 (CT SB 3, NY SAFE for Kids), **Stored Communications Act / state wiretap for any DM scanning**, CAN-SPAM on anything that is not transactional, and the state privacy regimes on whichever thresholds the client crosses. **Two build rules that are compliance, not craft: a machine never closes an account** (it may hold, hide and queue — a human decides), and **NOTICE AND REASON ARE SEPARABLE** — tell the author the post was actioned and how to appeal, but withhold the reason WORD for the categories where naming it tips off an investigation or is itself the attack (see `security-reviewer` A06). All of it to `compliance-counsel` **before** the first stranger can post.
**#1 failure:** shipping the publish path and deferring the enforcement path — a report button with nothing behind it, a guidelines page describing a ladder the code does not implement, a queue nobody is staffed to work, or a "we review everything" promise. The second failure, and the one that actually shipped: **an enforcement path that is itself an abuse weapon** — one unverified report from any account causing the platform to accuse another person, in the platform's own voice, on their lock screen. Enumerate those before launch; §4b's user-path walk will not find them. **The third, and the one that eats the retention half of this vertical: CUSTOMIZATION THEATRE** — a rich editor whose toggles produce nothing on the rendered page, sitting next to a Follow button that does not follow and a tablist that is two `role="tab"` buttons with no panel. Whyza shipped all three at once on a live profile. It is the identity-surface twin of the report button with nothing behind it: the product invites a person to express themselves, records the choice, and renders none of it — and because the switch flips and the state saves, no typecheck, test, lint or console will ever mention it.

**Compliance footnote specific to profiles:** a public contact affordance is a scraping and harassment vector. Any email or phone on a public profile must be authenticated-only or obfuscated — never plain text served to `anon`. And a self-selected "account type" must read as **self-described**, never "verified", and never a checkmark that mimics one.

## 13. Coin / bullion / collectibles dealer who already sells on marketplaces (eBay · Whatnot live shows)
*(Added 2026-10-10 from Lit on Fire Coins: a one-person dealer doing ~$1M/yr on eBay and Whatnot, run from a phone. Read §11 first; this is its quantity-1 model plus three things §11 does not have: precious-metals payment rules, live-show commerce, and a marketplace that already holds his reputation.)*
**Archetype:** **B — editorial commerce**, phone-first; the owner works the store from his phone, so the dashboard is a phone product too.
**Money moment:** a buyer who found him on eBay or a Whatnot show buys a specific coin direct, trusting it is the same seller, with no marketplace in between.
**Above the fold:** the coins, photographed, priced, plus whether he is live on Whatnot right now and when the next show is.
**Required:** every listing imported from his real store and re-checked against it at go-live (sold items removed) · auction listings never show a Buy button · bullion either sold or explicitly linked out to his eBay listing · returns terms that mirror his existing marketplace terms word for word · a live-now signal that is automatic (servers get refused by Whatnot; see memory `litonfire-auto-live-checker`).
**Trust proof:** his REAL marketplace feedback, copied from his public eBay/Whatnot profiles with the date read, and his own listing descriptions. Run descriptions through a **strict claim filter**: drop rarity, investment, authenticity-guarantee and guarantee sentences and any grade not in the listing title. Never write "insured", "graded" or "certified" unless he has confirmed it. In this vertical one overclaim reads as a counterfeit tell.
**Schema:** `Product` + `Offer` (`itemCondition`, `availability` → `SoldOut`), `Store`, `FAQPage`, and `MerchantReturnPolicy` built from the same terms as the returns page.
**Booking/payment:** the client's OWN Stripe account (Elevare invited as a team member), themed in-site Elements. **Precious metals / bullion are a restricted category on Stripe: get written approval before any bullion goes through checkout.** Until then, route bullion to his eBay listings and count the clicks. Elevare's fee covers website sales only (marketplace sales excluded), invoiced monthly in arrears.
**Compliance:** precious-metals processor approval (above) · state sales tax on coins and bullion varies and is the client's, with an accountant · shipping insurance is a carrier/label setting, so confirm what his label provider actually does before any copy mentions it · privacy and terms before the first stored address · route to `compliance-counsel` before go-live.
**#1 failure:** a site that competes with his marketplace reputation instead of borrowing it: invented reviews, investment language, "certified" claims or a stock-photo catalog, aimed at buyers who have spent years learning to spot exactly that.
