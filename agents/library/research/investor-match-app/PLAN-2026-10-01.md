## 1. The job and the promise

1. **The job:** a free public page and posting home for every business (what they make, where they are, their hours), where the same verified record lets businesses that want capital find backers who fit, and talk only when both say yes.
2. **Source and rank:** the owner's own words, 2026-10-01: "Next app social media app that matches businesses with investors"; "Both and we charge to get the matching as this could change businesses lives"; and "Well also a social media for business they can post what they make where there at there hours" (the last two relayed by the coordinator). Both reports are agent research, ranked below his words. "Charge to get the matching" ships as "membership includes matching" (legal 6A.3); decision D1 asks him to confirm.
3. **The promise (draft for brand-copywriter; counsel reviews):** "Your business, kept current: what you make, where you are, when you're open. When you're ready to grow, meet the backers who fit you, and talk only when you both say yes. Membership is the same price whether or not you raise a dollar, and we never take a cut of any deal."
4. **Definition of done for v1:** a business publishes a useful public page and posts from an iPhone in under ten minutes, and a verified member business can finish a packet, see fitting backers with reasons, send a request, and reach an open conversation with a backer who accepted, on iPhone and web, with every moderation control live.
5. **Challenge to the brief (SHARED-RULES 11):** only **2% of small employer firms took any equity** in a year and **60% applied for financing, mostly debt** [M]. The one capital path legal in v1 without a licence (equity matching to accredited backers) is the door Main Street rarely uses. The owner's page addition is what makes Main Street worth serving in v1: the page is useful with zero backers. The plan does not sell Main Street a match it cannot deliver; lender matching waits for decision D2.
6. **Second challenge:** every business already has free alternatives (Google Business Profile, Instagram, Nextdoor). A second page they must keep current is a chore unless it does something those cannot. Section 2.3 names that thing; if the pilot shows owners will not keep a second page current, the page shrinks to a profile and the plan says so.
7. **The one question that changes what gets built:** *Which of your five active clients has asked you about raising or borrowing money in the past year, how much, and for what?* Loans move the v2 lender path forward; none means the startup lane is the real first paying market and the page carries Main Street alone.

## 2. What the app is, and is NOT

### 2.1 It is two layers on one account
1. **Public layer (business life):** a page per business with what it makes or sells, photos, where it is (address or service area, map), hours with special days, and posts. Anyone can read it, on the web and in the app, signed out. It never carries capital content.
2. **Members layer (capital):** profiles of accredited backers, a readiness packet, matching on plain facts, introduction requests, and a private thread that opens only when both say yes. Members only, never indexed.

Elevare is paid a flat membership fee for software in the members layer. The public layer is free.

### 2.2 It is NOT
1. A broker. No fee per match, introduction, meeting or acceptance; no success fee, percentage, equity, warrant or after-deal payment, even if offered.
2. An offering venue. No raise amount, valuation, instrument, minimum, terms, deal room or "commit" button anywhere, and **never on the public layer in any form, including "investors welcome" or "open to investment" banners**.
3. A bank, escrow or shop. It never touches investment, loan or customer money.
4. An adviser. No score, rank, grade, "top deals" or AI opinion on investing. The AI refuses merit questions.
5. A lender matcher in v1. Lenders and CDFIs appear only as free directory information.
6. A crowdfunding portal. Community rounds are not on it.
7. A data broker. Every page and profile is created by its owner; no scraped or imported lists (Apple 5.1.1(viii)), including no pages auto-created from Google listings.
8. A review site. No star ratings or customer reviews in v1.
9. Open to under-18 accounts or non-US members.

Paywall and pricing-page disclaimer [P, 6A.3]: "Membership buys software access: matching tools, the packet, messaging and introduction requests to members who opt in. Your price is the same whether or not you raise money. ELEVARESTUDIO LLC is not a broker-dealer, funding portal, investment adviser or lender and receives no compensation from any investment, loan or financing between members."

### 2.3 Why this is not a worse Instagram, Google Business Profile or Nextdoor
| | Instagram | Google Business Profile | Nextdoor | This app |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Who controls the record | Owner, but no structured hours or place | Google; edits by the public; reviews by strangers | Neighbours; businesses pay for reach | The owner alone, verified |
| Hours and place as structured truth | No | Yes | Partial | Yes, with special days and "last confirmed" date |
| Reach | Algorithm, paid boosts | Search | Paid ads | Followers and local discovery; **reach is never sold** |
| What the page is worth beyond customers | Nothing | Nothing | Nothing | **The same record a backer reads: hours kept current, posting consistency and verified facts, shown as dated facts, never a score** |

The hook: the page a business keeps for customers becomes its track record. A backer sees a business that has kept its hours current for 14 months and posted 40 times, from the business's own hand, not a pitch deck. That is the one thing the three alternatives structurally cannot offer.

## 3. Who uses it, and what each does on day one

| Role | Day one | Pays? |
|---|---|---|
| **Business owner (Main Street)** | Email code sign-in, 18+, identity check, builds the public page (what they make, photos, address or service area, hours), first post, share link; sees the readiness checklist, "which money fits you," the free lender and CDFI directory, and how many backers fit | Free page and posting; Member for matching |
| **Business owner (startup)** | Same page, plus the investor-ready packet and fitting backers | Free page; Member for matching |
| **Accredited backer** | Sign-up, 18+, identity check, accredited self-certification (one attestation and a basis category, no figures, no documents), states sectors, stages, lanes, regions and cheque band; browses public pages; follows businesses privately; accepts, declines or sends requests | Always free |
| **Public visitor** | Reads pages and posts, gets directions, taps through to call or visit the website, reports anything signed out | No account in v1 |
| **Moderator (Elevare, the owner at first)** | Works the queue, image holds, notices, appeals | n/a |
| **Lender / CDFI** | Not a role in v1; directory listing only | n/a |

**Audience of posts (recommendation for v1): the public, read-only.** Why: (1) the page is only single-player value if customers can see it, which is what solves cold start; (2) indexable pages give local search and AI-answer discovery, the thing owners already pay for; (3) a public audience forces the cleanest legal line, because nothing capital-related can ever appear there. Interaction (follow, save) needs an account, and v1 accounts are businesses and backers only. Customer accounts are v1.1 after counsel Q10, and would never see the members layer.

**Public pages are indexable on the web twin** (`/b/[slug]`), with `LocalBusiness` JSON-LD, sitemap entries and share images. Members routes are `noindex` and AI-crawler disallowed (two separate mechanisms, both shipped).

**Accredited self-certification:** one attestation ("I am an accredited investor under Rule 501(a)") plus a basis category; Elevare verifies identity only, through a pass or fail vendor, and stores no ID; the badge never says "accredited verified" [P, 2.3, 8.3, 8.5]. Issuers keep their own verification duty. A third-party accreditation credential is LATER.

**Keeping non-accredited people from turning it into an offering venue:**
1. The public layer has no capital fields and a filter that blocks capital language in every post, caption and page field.
2. Member accounts are businesses and accredited-attested backers only; there is no community or customer account in v1.
3. Messaging exists only inside a business to backer connection.
4. Backer follows are private and never counted publicly ("followed by 12 investors" would read as endorsement of an offering).
5. Violations are removed with notice and appeal. Counsel confirms the split is enough (Q10).

## 6. Matching rules

### 6.1 Inputs (objective facts each person typed in; enums only)
| Business | Backer |
|---|---|
| Lane: Main Street or startup (capital profile) | Lanes considered |
| Category, from the public page (fixed list of about 20) | Sectors (same list) |
| State and metro, from the public page | Regions: metros or states |
| Stage: idea, pre-revenue, early revenue, established (capital profile) | Stages considered |
| Open to investor conversations (members only) | Open to requests |
| Years trading band; team size band | Cheque band (enum) |

**Never inputs:** amount raising, valuation, instrument, terms, revenue figures, returns, a visible "funding type sought" field (Roommates.com problem, legal 8.4). **The page signals (hours confirmed, post count, member since) are shown to backers as dated facts but are never inputs to matching or ordering** (that would turn activity into a merit grade); counsel confirms in Q5.

### 6.2 How a match is computed
A backer fits a business when **all four** hold: lane is in the backer's lanes; category is in the backer's sectors; location is inside the backer's regions; stage is in the backer's stages. Both have "open" on, neither has blocked the other, both identities passed, and the backer's attestation is current. Same rule, same code, for every member. No weights, no learning, no AI.

### 6.3 What each person sees
1. Everyone who fits, each with a reason line: "Fits because: same sector (Food and drink), same metro (Hartford), a stage you consider (Established), a lane you consider (Main Street)."
2. Neutral order: most recently active first, with filters. No score, "best" or "top."
3. Free tier: the count ("7 backers fit your stated facts") and the reason categories; profiles open on Member.
4. Zero fits: what would widen it ("no backer in your metro considers your stage yet") plus the directory. Never a dead end.
5. A backer opening a business sees its public page facts in a strip: "Hours last confirmed 3 days ago · 41 posts in 14 months · Member since 2026 · Identity verified · Registration found in Connecticut."

### 6.4 Rate limits (same for every member and tier, never sold)
1. Requests: 5 new per rolling 7 days; 10 pending at once; 14-day expiry; one request per pair per 90 days after a decline.
2. Posts: 5 per day per business (anti-spam, same for all).
3. Enforced by atomic SQL functions, not app code.

## 8. Trust and safety

### 8.1 Verification
1. Email code, 18+ attestation, identity vendor pass. **No pass, no public posting and no requests** (the strongest single deterrent for a one-person moderation team).
2. Business: state registration number checked against the public state registry. Badge text: "Identity verified · Registration found in [State]." Never "vetted," "trusted" or "approved."
3. Backer: accredited attestation, renewed yearly.

### 8.2 The pre-post check (composer and every public field)
1. **Layer 1, deterministic text classifier** (no model): blocks raise amounts with raise words, valuation, pre-money, SAFE, convertible note, equity offered, returns, "invest in us," "investors wanted," "open to investment," crowdfunding pitches; plus slurs and contact-harvesting patterns. Hard hit: refused with a plain explanation and a link to how raises lawfully happen. Soft hit: published to the author only, held for review.
2. **Layer 2, optional model check** on held items only (gateway, refusal control).
3. **Images:** known-CSAM hash matching before publish (vendor chosen in wave 3 by security-reviewer and ai-engineer; NCMEC reporting path ready), plus an adult and violence classifier; anything unchecked within its window stays held, never auto-published.
4. Photos showing people: the composer asks "Do you have permission from everyone shown?" (INDUSTRIES 2 and 12 photo consent).

### 8.3 Moderation (Apple 1.2, INDUSTRIES 12)
1. Report on every page, post, image, profile, request, message and packet share, reachable signed out.
2. Block hides both people from each other everywhere.
3. Review queue in the console: reason, reporter count, check results, history on one screen. Response target 48 hours [E]; publish only what the owner can staff.
4. Notice to the author on every action, with an appeal path and a 14-day deadline; a written strike ladder that matches the code.
5. A machine never suspends an account; it may hold and queue. A human decides.
6. One unverified report never shows an accusation on anyone's screen.
7. NCMEC ESP registration started before launch (only the owner can file; turnaround is the cost); one-year preservation. DMCA agent registered and published.
8. Load estimate [E]: 1,000 businesses at 8 posts a month is 8,000 posts; at a 1% hold-or-report rate, about 3 queue items a day. At 10,000 businesses, about 30 a day: a part-time moderator is a cost line before that row.

### 8.4 Bad actor checks
1. Businesses attest in the terms that no Rule 506(d) disqualifying event applies [P, 8.2].
2. OFAC question for counsel.
3. Signals queued for a human: repeated identity fails, repeated filter hits, many reports, many declines, a new account at the request limit in its first hour.

### 8.5 Terms and disclaimers (compliance-counsel drafts from the built code)
The eight clauses in legal 8.6; the paywall disclaimer; badge wording; content and photo-consent rules; DMCA notice; auto-renewal disclosure; CAN-SPAM footer; privacy policy from the shipped data inventory; governing law Connecticut; entity ELEVARESTUDIO LLC exactly.

## 10. The manual test BEFORE code

**Precondition:** a securities lawyer reads this section and answers Q1, Q5, Q10 and Q11 in writing before the first member is recruited.

### 10.1 Shape (one metro, 8 weeks)
1. **20 businesses** (target 12 Main Street, 8 startup) and **50 backers** (accredited equity only: angels, angel group members, small funds).
2. **Pages first, no app code:** each business sends photos, what they make, location and hours through a form. Elevare publishes one static pilot directory (studio default: plain HTML from a sheet, `noindex` during the pilot) with a page per business. Each week the business sends at least one post through the form; the owner publishes it unedited after the filter check. Pages are free.
3. **Matching by hand, by the published rule:** members fill the section 6.1 fields on a form. Each Monday the owner runs the section 6.2 rule in a spreadsheet, unchanged for everyone, and emails each member the list of people who fit them, with reasons and a link to each business page. He adds nothing, recommends nothing, and never contacts a backer about a specific business.
4. A member replies "yes" to any name. When both have said yes, both get each other's email in one message. Elevare steps out.
5. **Price:** pages free; matching is a flat **$29.99 founding membership** on Square, cancel any time, same price whether or not anyone connects. Backers free. Main Street members also get the lender-ready packet template and the CDFI and SBA Lender Match links.
6. **Lane floor:** no business in a lane is billed until that lane has 15 active backers in the metro.

### 10.2 Script
1. **Business invitation:** "We'll put your business on a free page: what you make, where you are, when you're open, and your posts. When you want to grow, members can see which local backers fit them and talk only when both say yes. Membership is $29.99 a month, the same whether or not you raise a dollar, and we take nothing from any deal."
2. **Business interview (20 minutes):** "Where do customers find your hours today? How often do you post, and where? In the next six months, will you need money you don't have? For what? Who have you asked? What would you need in hand to walk into a lender or investor tomorrow?"
3. **Backer recruiting (to the network, never to a company):** "A members-only network of local businesses in [metro], each with a page they keep themselves. You list what you look at; each Monday you get the businesses whose own facts fit yours. You connect only if both say yes. Free for backers, we take no part of any deal, and nobody will pitch you on Elevare's behalf. Accredited investors only; you'll confirm that once."
4. **Never said:** "we'll find you investors," "guaranteed introductions," "get funded," "only pay when," any raise amount or terms.

### 10.3 The numbers (cold start first, then the three continue-or-stop numbers)
0. **Cold-start number (the page):** at least 12 of 20 businesses send a post in 6 of 8 weeks and confirm their hours at least monthly. **If under 5, the page shrinks to a profile in v1** (owners will not keep a second page).
1. **Mutual yes rate:** at least 30% of business yeses met by a backer yes within 14 days. **Stop or rebuild supply under 15%.** (The market's 50% assumed hand-picked introductions; a rule-computed list is wider [E].)
2. **Willingness to pay a flat membership:** at least 8 of 20 pay in month one and 6 still pay in month two. **Stop if fewer than 3.**
3. **Life-changing outcome:** at least 3 of 20 report a real step within 120 days (a second meeting, a loan from a directory lender, an angel cheque, a raise launched on a registered portal). **Stop if zero.** Self-reported, never a condition of any fee.

Guard rail: more than 5 owner hours a week before number 3 is hit means pause.
