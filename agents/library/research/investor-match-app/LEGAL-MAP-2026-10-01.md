## 0. Bottom line in five lines

1. The legal risk is in one word of the brief: **"matches."** A directory where people find each other is software. A service that brings a specific investor to a specific raise, and gets paid when money moves, is a broker. Everything in this map is about staying on the software side of that line.
2. **Never take money that depends on whether, or how much, anyone invests.** No success fee, no percentage, no carry, no per-introduction fee, no "boost my raise" fee. Flat, outcome-blind subscriptions are the only revenue model this report treats as low risk for an unlicensed company.
3. **No offering terms on the platform in v1.** No raise amount, valuation, instrument, minimums, deal rooms, commitments or money flow. The moment a company's raise is visible on a feed, the company is generally soliciting, and the platform starts to look like the "platform or mechanism" for an offering.
4. **No "this is a good investment" logic.** Search and filters on objective facts the users typed in are fine. Scores, rankings or AI picks about investment merit are investment advice.
5. **Apple will read this as a finance app unless the build and the metadata make it plainly a professional network.** Guideline 3.2.1(viii) says investing apps must come from a licensed financial institution. Submit as ELEVARESTUDIO LLC (organisation account), category Business or Social Networking, 18+, full UGC moderation, and a review note that says exactly what the app does not do.

**Challenge to the brief (SHARED-RULES section 11).** Source rank: the owner's own words, one sentence, no detail. Two things in that sentence change the product:
- "businesses" is open. Elevare's client base is local small businesses (salons, trades, shops). Those businesses rarely run Regulation D offerings with accredited angels. If the real user is a local business wanting money from local, non-accredited people, the only legal online route is Regulation Crowdfunding through a registered funding portal, which Elevare is not and should not try to become in v1.
- "matches" is the risk word above.

**The ONE question for the owner whose answer changes what gets built:** *Who is the investor: accredited angels and funds backing startups, or everyday people putting money into local businesses they know?* The first can be served by a network with no licence. The second cannot be served online without a funding portal or broker-dealer partner from day one.

**Owner's answer (relayed by the coordinator, 2026-10-01, his words):** "Both and we charge to get the matching as this could change businesses lives". So the app serves Main Street businesses **and** startups, and the revenue idea is charging for the matching itself. Section 6A answers exactly how matching can and cannot be charged for, and how serving Main Street (loans, revenue share, community rounds) moves the lines. Short version: **charging for matching is workable only as a flat membership that includes matching; charging per match, per introduction, or on any result is the finder fee the law is built to catch, and matching Main Street businesses with lenders or revenue-based funders adds state commercial-financing broker registration in both owner states.**

## 1. The broker-dealer line (Exchange Act section 15(a))

### 1.1 The rule
- Exchange Act section 15(a)(1) makes it unlawful for an unregistered "broker" to effect, or induce or attempt to induce, any securities transaction. A "broker" is a person "engaged in the business of effecting transactions in securities for the account of others" (section 3(a)(4)(A)); "engaged in the business" is not defined by statute or rule. **[P via petition text, see 1.3]**
- Consequence for the users, not just the platform: Exchange Act section 29(b) makes contracts made in violation of the Act void as to a party who knowingly violated it. A raise run through an unregistered broker can hand investors a rescission argument against the company. **[P] 15 U.S.C. 78cc(b)**

### 1.2 What the SEC says makes you a broker
The SEC's Guide to Broker-Dealer Registration (April 2008) asks four questions **[P]**:
1. Do you participate in important parts of a securities transaction, including **solicitation, negotiation, or execution**?
2. Does your compensation **depend on, or relate to, the outcome or size** of the transaction?
3. Are you otherwise engaged in the business of effecting or facilitating securities transactions?
4. Do you **handle the securities or funds** of others?

The same guide lists "finding investors for issuers" and operating "electronic or other platforms" among the activities that may require registration. **[P]**

### 1.3 Finders: what the SEC and courts have said
- **Transaction-based compensation is the "hallmark"** of a broker in SEC practice. The 1991 Paul Anka no-action letter allowed a narrow, one-off finder fee; in 2010 the staff refused relief to Brumberg, Mackey & Wall, confirming that transaction-based pay for introducing investors generally requires registration. **[S]** (as described in the De Silva Law Offices petition to the SEC of 2026-03-17, a primary filing but a partisan characterisation of the letters; the letters themselves were not read in this session)
- **Courts use a multi-factor test**, not a single factor: transaction-based compensation, participation at key points, negotiation, advice or valuation, handling funds, regular participation and active solicitation. SEC v. Kramer (M.D. Fla. 2011) rejected the SEC's attempt to make transaction-based compensation alone decisive. **[S]**
- The Eighth Circuit in SEC v. Collyard (2017) upheld broker liability for a person paid commissions for referring investors and rejected a broad federal "finder" exception. **[S]** (the petition says the Eighth Circuit "explicitly rejected any broad finders exemption as a matter of federal law")

### 1.4 Status of a federal finder exemption: NOT LAW
- The SEC proposed a conditional finder exemption on 2020-10-07 (Release 34-90112, File S7-13-20): two tiers of **natural persons only**, accredited investors only, written agreement, **no general solicitation**. **[S]** It was never adopted.
- The SEC's Small Business Capital Formation Advisory Committee put finders back on its agenda on 2025-07-22. **[P] sec.gov SBCFAC agenda**
- On 2026-03-17 a law firm petitioned Chairman Atkins to "revive" the 2020 proposal through full rulemaking, which confirms it was still not adopted on that date. **[P]**
- The Spring 2026 Regulatory Flexibility Agenda includes an item on the regulatory status of finders. **[S]** (Sidley via Harvard Law School Forum, 2026-08-18; the agenda entry's exact title and stage were not confirmed from reginfo.gov **[U]**)
- **Design consequence:** do not plan on a finder exemption. Even if one is adopted, the 2020 version covered individual people, not platforms, and barred general solicitation, which a social feed is.

### 1.5 The one statutory safe harbour for platforms, and why it does not fit a paid app
Securities Act section 4(c) (15 U.S.C. 77d(c); enacted by JOBS Act Title II as "4(b)" and still often called that) says a person who maintains "a platform or mechanism that permits the offer, sale, purchase, or negotiation of" Rule 506 securities, or permits general solicitation by issuers, is not a broker **solely because** of that, **if** **[P]**:
- (A) the person and every associated person "receives **no compensation** in connection with the purchase or sale of such security";
- (B) it does not have possession of customer funds or securities; and
- (C) it is not subject to a statutory disqualification.

It also allows "ancillary services": due diligence that is not "for separate compensation, investment advice or recommendations", and standardised documents, provided the platform does not negotiate terms for others. **[P]**

The SEC staff FAQ (2013-02-05) reads "compensation" **"broadly, to include any direct or indirect economic benefit,"** treats a **salary paid to staff for these activities as compensation**, and says the section **does not exempt anyone from state registration**. **[P]** The FAQ does not say whether a flat subscription is "compensation in connection with the purchase or sale." **[P, silent]**

**Design consequence:** a subscription business cannot comfortably rely on section 4(c). The safer path is to **not be a platform for the offer or sale of securities at all**: no offering terms, no deal mechanics, no money. Then section 4(c) is never needed. Whether a flat fee for a network that also hosts general business profiles is "in connection with" a sale is open question 1 for the lawyer.

## 2. General solicitation: Regulation D 506(b) versus 506(c)

### 2.1 The two lanes
- **506(b):** no general solicitation; unlimited accredited investors plus up to 35 non-accredited sophisticated investors.
- **506(c):** general solicitation allowed; **every purchaser must be accredited** and the issuer must take **reasonable steps to verify** it. Securities Act section 4(b) (15 U.S.C. 77d(b)) says 506 offerings are not public offerings "as a result of general advertising or general solicitation." **[P]**

### 2.2 What counts as general solicitation when a raise is on a social feed
- A public post that a company is raising, with terms or an invitation to invest, made to people the company has no **pre-existing, substantive relationship** with, is general solicitation. A 506(b) company that does this loses 506(b) for that round and must fall back to 506(c) (all-accredited, verified). **[S]** (SEC staff C&DIs and Citizen VC no-action letter, both 2015-08-06, as summarised by Skadden; the C&DI page on sec.gov could not be read in full in this session **[U for exact question numbers]**)
- **Factual business information** (what the company does, its products, its news) is not an offer if it is not presented in a way that offers securities. **[S]**
- A **pre-existing substantive relationship** can be built online, but a short self-certification questionnaire is **not enough** on its own; the relationship needs enough information to judge the person's finances and sophistication, and it must exist **before** the offering begins. **[S]** (Citizen VC, 2015)
- **Demo days (Rule 148):** communications at events run by universities, governments, non-profits, angel groups, incubators or accelerators are not general solicitation if the sponsor makes no recommendations, does no negotiation, charges only "reasonable administrative fees," takes no pay for introductions, and the offering information is limited to "a notification that the issuer is in the process of offering ... the type and amount of securities being offered, the intended use of proceeds ... and the unsubscribed amount"; online attendance must be limited to sponsor members, people reasonably believed accredited, or invitees chosen for investment experience. **[P] 17 CFR 230.148**. Elevare is not one of the listed sponsor types, but Rule 148 is a useful guide to what the SEC regards as "offering information."
- **Testing the waters (Rule 241)** lets an issuer use generic solicitation-of-interest materials before choosing an exemption; it carries integration conditions if the issuer then uses 506(b). **[S]** (2020 harmonisation release)

### 2.3 506(c) verification, current position
Rule 506(c)(2)(ii) non-exclusive methods **[P]**: (A) two years of IRS income forms plus a current-year representation; (B) asset and liability documents from the last three months; (C) written confirmation from a registered broker-dealer, SEC-registered investment adviser, licensed attorney or CPA within three months; (D) legacy pre-2013 506(b) investors; (E) a **previously verified** person's written representation, good for **five years** from the original verification.

**Self-certification plus high minimum (2025 no-action letter).** On **2025-03-12** the SEC Division of Corporation Finance told Latham & Watkins that an issuer "could reasonably conclude" it took reasonable steps where **[P]**:
1. minimum investment of at least **$200,000** for natural persons and **$1,000,000** for entities;
2. written representations that the purchaser is accredited and that the minimum "is not financed in whole or in part by any third party" for this investment; and
3. the issuer has "no actual knowledge" of facts to the contrary.

That route helps issuers, not Elevare. Elevare should **not** perform or sell accreditation verification in v1: method (C) is reserved for brokers, advisers, attorneys and CPAs, and collecting tax returns or brokerage statements would put financial records into Elevare's database against the house sensitive-data rule.

### 2.4 Does a "profiles only, no offering terms" platform change the analysis?
**Yes, substantially, but not completely.**
- It keeps the **platform** out of the "platform or mechanism that permits the offer, sale" language of section 4(c) and away from the funding-portal definition, because no offer is on it.
- It does **not** stop a **user** from generally soliciting through it. If a 506(b) founder posts "Raising $1.5M at $8M pre, DM me," that founder has generally solicited. The platform did not make the offer, but a product that invites such posts (a "Currently raising: $___" field) helps make it, which is the Roommates.com problem in section 8.4.
- Practical rules that follow:
  - No raise amount, valuation, instrument, minimum or closing date fields anywhere.
  - A neutral "open to investor conversations" toggle visible **only to investor accounts that have opted in and self-certified as accredited** is the most this report would put in v1, and only after counsel answers open question 2.
  - Community rules and automated filters that block posting of offering terms on public surfaces, with a short in-product explanation of why.

## 3. Regulation Crowdfunding: when a platform becomes a funding portal

- Reg CF lets a company raise up to **$5,000,000** in 12 months **[P] 17 CFR 227.100(a)(1)**. Non-accredited investor limits: if income or net worth is below **$124,000**, the greater of **$2,500** or **5%** of the greater of income or net worth; if both are at or above $124,000, **10%** of the greater, capped at **$124,000** **[P] 227.100(a)(2)**.
- The offering must be "conducted **exclusively through the intermediary's platform**," and the intermediary must be an SEC-registered broker-dealer or **funding portal** that is a FINRA member. **[P] 227.100(a)(3); sec.gov Reg CF page**
- A funding portal (Exchange Act section 3(a)(80)) is a broker acting solely under section 4(a)(6), and it **may not**: offer investment advice or recommendations; solicit purchases or sales of securities displayed on its platform; compensate anyone for such solicitation or based on sales; or hold, manage or handle investor funds or securities. **[S]** (statutory text as quoted by SEC sources in search results)
- The Rule 402 safe harbour lets a portal apply **objective criteria** to highlight offerings, offer search and sort by objective factors, run communication channels it does not participate in beyond moderation, and pay for referrals not tied to sales. **[P] 17 CFR 227.402(b)**
- An issuer may advertise a Reg CF offering **off** the portal only with a notice limited to the terms in Rule 204 that directs investors to the portal. **[P] 17 CFR 227.204**

**When Elevare's app would become a funding portal:** if it hosts Reg CF offerings, takes investment commitments, shows offering terms beyond what an issuer's Rule 204 notice allows, or is where the transaction is "conducted." **What a non-portal may not do:** host or run any Reg CF offering, accept commitments, direct investor money, or present itself as the place to invest. Registering as a portal is SEC Form Funding Portal plus FINRA membership: a regulated business, not a feature.

**What a non-portal may do:** let a company link out to its live campaign on a registered portal. Even that should wait for counsel (open question 4), because the link plus the app's own promotion could be read as solicitation.

## 4. Investment adviser risk: when matches become advice

- **Federal definition:** an investment adviser is a person who, for compensation, is in the business of advising others about the value of securities or the advisability of investing in them (Advisers Act 202(a)(11)). The SEC and NASAA's Release IA-1092 (1987) reads all three elements broadly: advice about investing "in general," "in the business" does not need to be the main business, and compensation is any economic benefit. **[S]** (the sec.gov PDF is a scanned image and could not be text-extracted **[U for quotations]**)
- **State definitions** in both owner states match: Connecticut 36b-3 and Virginia 13.1-501 define an investment adviser as one who, for compensation, advises others "as to the value of securities or ... the advisability of investing in, purchasing or selling securities." **[P]**
- **Publisher exclusion:** Lowe v. SEC (1985) protects bona fide publications of **impersonal** advice of general and regular circulation. **[S]** A personalised match ("this startup fits **you**") is the opposite of impersonal; do not plan on the exclusion.
- **A useful federal analogue for "objective":** the Reg CF Rule 402(b) safe harbour allows highlighting and search using **objective criteria** applied consistently. **[P]** That is the line to design against:

| Matching feature | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Filter by fields both sides entered (industry, stage, city, stated cheque size, stated thesis keywords) | Search tool. OK. |
| "Suggested" list ordered by overlap of those stated fields, with the reason shown ("same industry, same city") | Low risk if the criteria are objective, disclosed and the same for everyone. Confirm with counsel. |
| Any score, grade, rank or badge about a company's quality, traction, valuation, risk or expected return | Advice. Do not ship. |
| AI summary that comments on merit ("strong team, undervalued") or answers "should I invest?" | Advice. Do not ship. The AI must refuse investment-merit questions. |
| Paid placement at the top of investor feeds | Advice plus solicitation risk. Do not ship. |

- **Status notes:** the SEC's 2023 predictive-data-analytics proposal for advisers and brokers was **withdrawn on 2025-06-12** **[S]**, so no pending AI-specific rule applies; the general definition still does. FinCEN's investment-adviser AML rule is postponed to **2028-01-01** **[S]**; it only matters if Elevare ever registers as an adviser.

## 6. Monetisation

**Safe (low risk, still confirm with counsel):**
1. Flat subscription for investors (same price regardless of activity or outcomes).
2. Flat subscription for businesses for profile, messaging and software tools.
3. Software features priced per seat or per month: CRM, pipeline tracking of the user's own contacts, deck hosting with view analytics, calendar, notes.
4. Event tickets for educational events at a flat price (if a pitch event is ever run, follow Rule 148 limits and get counsel first).
5. Sponsorship or advertising from **non-securities** businesses (law firms, accountants, software), clearly labelled.

**Not safe for an unlicensed company:**
1. Success fees, percentage of amount raised, retainer-plus-success structures.
2. Equity, warrants or options taken from businesses on the platform, or carried interest.
3. Per-introduction, per-meeting or per-investor-contact fees.
4. Prices that scale with the size of the raise, or refunds if a raise fails (both make the fee outcome-linked).
5. Paid placement or "boost" for a company's raise; paid investor outreach on a company's behalf.

Rail note: in-app subscriptions go through Apple and Google billing (section 7); a web subscription for Elevare's own product runs on **Square** per house rules. Neither rail touches investment money, ever.

## 6A. Charging for the matching itself (owner decision, 2026-10-01)

### 6A.1 The test every fee design is held to
Four sources draw the line, and they agree:
- **SEC Guide to Broker-Dealer Registration** asks whether compensation "depend[s] upon, or is it related to, the **outcome or size** of the transaction," and lists "finding investors for issuers" as broker activity. **[P]**
- **Section 4(c) and the SEC FAQ:** the platform safe harbour needs "**no compensation** in connection with the purchase or sale," read "broadly, to include any direct or indirect economic benefit." **[P]**
- **Rule 148 (demo days):** even an approved sponsor of an investor event may charge only "reasonable administrative fees" and may not "receive any compensation for **making introductions** between event attendees and issuers." **[P] 17 CFR 230.148.** This is the clearest primary statement that the SEC treats **pay for introductions** as different in kind from pay for access or administration.
- **State finder rules define the finder by pay for introductions:** California 25206.1 covers a person who introduces accredited investors to issuers for compensation **[P]**; Texas defines a finder as one who receives compensation "for introducing an accredited investor to an issuer **or an issuer to an accredited investor**" (both directions) and requires registration and bans advertising **[P/S]**. Neither regime is open to a company.
- **Courts** look at the whole picture (SEC v. Kramer, 2011): transaction-based pay, solicitation, negotiation, advice, handling funds, regularity. A business whose revenue rises with each introduction it makes is "regular" by design. **[S]** Collyard (8th Cir. 2017) shows that pay per referred investor is enough for liability when the referrer is in the business of referring. **[S]**

Labels do not change substance: calling a per-introduction charge a "software fee" does not make it one if the price moves with introductions.

### 6A.2 The five ways to charge for matching

| # | Model | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(a)** | Flat subscription that **includes** matching (same price whether the member gets zero introductions or fifty, whether anyone invests or not) | **SAFE, lowest risk available; counsel to confirm (open question 1)** | Not tied to outcome, size or the number of introductions. It is the Gust model (subscription, "IS NOT A BROKER, DEALER OR INVESTMENT ADVISOR") **[P]** and matches angel-group membership dues, which Rule 148 treats as a normal sponsor type **[P]**. Residual risk: section 4(c) cannot be relied on if any fee is "compensation in connection with" a sale, which is why v1 keeps offerings off the platform entirely. |
| **(b)** | Flat fee to **unlock or request** an introduction, paid whether or not anything follows | **GREY, do not ship in v1** | Not success-contingent, so it is not classic transaction-based compensation. But it is compensation **per introduction**, which is exactly what Rule 148 excludes and what California and Texas define as finder activity. Revenue scales with introductions, so regularity is built in. A good-faith argument exists (automated, no negotiation, no advice, no money), but it has to be a written lawyer opinion before launch, not a product assumption. |
| **(c)** | Per-match or per-introduction fee paid by the **business** | **NOT SAFE** | An issuer paying for each investor it is introduced to is the finder fee in its plainest form (the Paul Anka and Brumberg line, and Collyard). If the charge only applies when the investor accepts, it is also contingent on a step toward the transaction, which edges into outcome-based pay. |
| **(d)** | Per-match or per-introduction fee paid by the **investor** | **NOT SAFE in v1 (grey at best)** | Texas defines the finder in both directions, and section 4(c) bars compensation from anyone. Investor-paid deal-flow access is defensible only as flat membership (model (a)); priced per introduction it is a finder fee paid by the other side. |
| **(e)** | Any fee contingent on a **meeting, a term sheet, a closing or money raised** (success fee, percentage, "only pay when you get funded") | **NOT SAFE, never without a broker-dealer licence** | The "hallmark" of a broker: compensation that depends on the outcome or size of the transaction. Also makes Elevare a person "paid remuneration for solicitation of purchasers," a covered person under Rule 506(d) **[P]**, and gives investors a rescission argument against the business under Exchange Act 29(b) **[P]**. The only success-style referral pay this research found in lending (California Fin. Code 22602) is lender-paid, only on a consummated commercial loan at or under 36% APR, with underwriting, records and annual reports, and it bars the referrer from negotiating, advising or handling documents **[P]**; it does not cover securities and does not let a platform charge the business. |

### 6A.3 How to word and structure model (a) so it stays model (a)
**Structure**
1. One flat price per tier per period. Tiers may differ by **software features** (seats, saved searches, analytics, CRM size), never by raise size, amount sought, number of introductions, or outcomes.
2. Introduction requests are **included and not separately priced**. If anti-spam limits are needed, apply the same limit to every member as a rate limit, not as purchasable credits. No "buy more intros" pack.
3. **Mutual opt-in** only: an introduction opens when both sides accept. Matching ranks by overlap of **objective, user-stated fields**, shows the reason, and is the same algorithm for everyone (section 4).
4. Elevare staff never hand-pick, pitch or chase investors for a business ("concierge intros"); salary for that work is compensation under the SEC FAQ **[P]**.
5. No refunds, credits or discounts tied to whether a member gets funded, meets an investor, or receives a term sheet.
6. No fee, equity, warrant, revenue share or "thank-you" payment from any member after a deal closes, even if offered voluntarily. Terms say so.
7. Do not build pricing, sales bonuses or marketing targets on "money raised through the app."

**Wording (pricing page, store listing, terms)**
- Use: "Membership: access to [App]'s profiles, search, matching tools, messaging and introduction requests to members who opt in." "Your membership price is the same whether or not you raise money." "[App] is software. ELEVARESTUDIO LLC is not a broker-dealer, funding portal, investment adviser or lender and receives no compensation from any investment, loan or financing between members."
- Do not use: "pay per match," "pay per intro," "unlock investors," "we find you investors," "guaranteed introductions," "get funded," "only pay when you raise," "success plan," "raise faster with Elevare," or any "raised $X through [App]" claim. The owner's own phrase "charge to get the matching" should become "membership includes matching," never "charge per match."
- In-app, beside the paywall: one plain sentence that the subscription buys software access, not introductions to any particular investor or any financing outcome.

### 6A.4 Does serving Main Street alongside Regulation D startups change the lines?
**The securities lines do not move; they get harder to hold, and a second, non-securities licensing regime appears.**

1. **Startups raising under Regulation D:** everything in sections 1 to 4 applies unchanged.
2. **Main Street loans (term loans, lines of credit, equipment finance):** an ordinary commercial loan from a lender is usually **not a security** under Reves v. Ernst & Young (1990), whose list of non-security notes includes "short-term notes secured by small business liens or assets" and "character" loans to bank customers; the test starts from a presumption that every note is a security **[P/S]**. So securities broker rules often fall away, **but lending-broker laws take their place:**
   - **Connecticut** (home state): a "commercial financing broker" is a person who, "for compensation or the expectation of compensation, obtains or offers to obtain commercial financing for a recipient from a provider"; providers and brokers had to register with the Banking Commissioner through NMLS by **2024-10-01** (Conn. Gen. Stat. 36a-861 to 36a-872). **[P]** for the registration page, **[S]** for the definition text and for the reported $250,000 scope.
   - **Virginia:** a "sales-based financing broker" is a person that, "for compensation or the expectation of compensation, obtains or offers to obtain sales-based financing from a provider for a recipient"; registration with the SCC (**$1,000** initial, **$500** annual by September 15); the chapter does not apply to a single transaction over **$500,000** (Va. Code 6.2-2228 to 6.2-2230). **[P]**
   - **California:** the California Financing Law treats as a "broker" anyone engaged in the business of negotiating or performing any act as broker in connection with loans by a finance lender; the only unlicensed route is the narrow, lender-paid, consummation-only referral of Fin. Code 22602. **[P]** (22602) / **[S]** (broker definition, licence requirement)
   - **Consequence:** in Connecticut and Virginia the broker definition turns on compensation for **offering to obtain** financing, with no requirement that the pay be success-based. A flat membership whose advertised value is "get matched with lenders" could arguably be compensation for offering to obtain financing. **Matching businesses with lenders, merchant cash advance or revenue-based funders is therefore GREY even under model (a)** and should stay out of v1 unless counsel clears it state by state (open question 9). Other states have similar commercial-financing laws (New York, Utah, Georgia, Florida, Texas and others are commonly cited) **[U]**.
3. **Revenue-share agreements with individual investors:** usually an **investment contract**, so a security, under Howey (1946): "a person invests his money in a common enterprise and is led to expect profits solely from the efforts of the promoter or a third party" **[P]**. Every securities line applies. A revenue share sold by a business to a funder company may instead be sales-based financing under the Virginia and Connecticut laws above.
4. **Community rounds** (customers and neighbours buying equity, notes or revenue shares): securities, usually sold to **non-accredited** people, usually promoted publicly. The lawful online routes are **Reg CF through a registered funding portal** (section 3) or a **state intrastate exemption**: Virginia's allows up to **$2,000,000** in 12 months, **$10,000** per non-accredited purchaser, Virginia residents only, sold through the issuer's own site, a registered broker-dealer or a compliant portal that may not be paid based on sales (21 VAC 5-40-190) **[P]**. No Connecticut intrastate crowdfunding exemption was found; a 2014 to 2016 study is the last trace **[S/U]**. The app cannot be the place a community round is offered.
5. **What changes in practice when both audiences share one app:**
   - The **accredited-investor attestation gate** stops working as a filter, because Main Street's natural investors are not accredited. Decide per role: in v1, only accredited-attested accounts can receive investment-related introductions; community members can follow, review and buy from businesses but cannot be routed into investment conversations (open question 10).
   - Main Street owners are more likely to post "raising $50k, invest with us" publicly to non-accredited neighbours, which is an illegal public offering for them and a moderation duty for Elevare. Filters and community rules on offering terms (section 2.4) carry more weight, and the in-product explanation should point them to a registered portal.
   - Profiles must not carry a "funding type" field that implies Elevare arranges loans or revenue-based funding (Roommates.com required-field problem, section 8.4).

**Net for v1:** charge one flat membership that includes matching; match businesses with **equity investors only** (accredited, mutual opt-in, no terms on platform); leave lenders, revenue-based funders and community rounds to a later phase with a registered portal, a licensed lender or broker partner, and state-by-state counsel sign-off.

## 8. Other duties

### 8.1 Anti-fraud (Rule 10b-5 and section 17(a))
- The **issuer** is primarily liable for its own statements. Under Janus Capital (2011) only the "maker" of a statement is liable under Rule 10b-5(b), but under **Lorenzo v. SEC (2019-03-27)** anyone who **disseminates** false statements **with intent to defraud** can be liable under Rules 10b-5(a) and (c) without being the maker. **[S]**
- Platform exposure therefore comes from (a) Elevare's **own** words (marketing that says companies are "vetted," "verified," "top deals") and (b) knowingly amplifying a user's fraud. A "Verified" badge must say exactly what was verified ("identity verified by [vendor]"), never imply the business claims were checked.

### 8.2 Bad actor disqualification
- Rule 506(d) bars an issuer from 506 if a covered person has a disqualifying event; covered persons include "**any person that has been or will be paid (directly or indirectly) remuneration for solicitation of purchasers**." **[P] 17 CFR 230.506(d)(1)** Rule 506(e) requires disclosure of pre-2013 events. **[S]**
- Two consequences: (1) if Elevare is ever paid to solicit, Elevare and its officers become covered persons for every issuer it helps; (2) section 4(c) requires the platform and its associated persons to be free of statutory disqualification. Keep Elevare unpaid for solicitation, and have each issuer attest in the terms that it is not a bad actor (an attestation, not a verification).

### 8.3 KYC and identity
- No customer identification programme applies to a non-broker, non-adviser software company that never handles funds (FinCEN's adviser AML rule is postponed to 2028 and would not apply anyway). **[S]**
- OFAC sanctions rules apply to all US persons. **[U]** (not re-read in this session; confirm with counsel)
- **Expectation, not law:** investors want to know the founder is real and founders want to know the "investor" is not a scammer. Use an identity-verification **vendor** that returns pass or fail; **never store government ID images or numbers** (STANDARDS "Sensitive data" rule). The vendor becomes a row in the data inventory.

### 8.4 Section 230 for user posts
- 47 U.S.C. 230(c)(1): a provider is not treated "as the publisher or speaker of any information provided by another information content provider." An information content provider is anyone "responsible, in whole or in part, for the creation or development" of the content. 230(e)(1) leaves **federal criminal** law untouched. **[P]**
- **Fair Housing Council v. Roommates.com (9th Cir. 2008):** a site lost immunity for questions and drop-downs it **required** that elicited unlawful content; free-text "Additional Comments" stayed immune. **[S]** Translation: a required "Amount raising / valuation / expected return" field would make Elevare a co-developer of offering content. Do not build those fields.
- Whether section 230 limits **SEC civil enforcement** or **state securities regulators** is unsettled and was not researched to a primary source. **[U]** Open question 6.

### 8.5 Privacy (financial information as sensitive data)
- **Connecticut (home state):** CTDPA amendments effective **2026-07-01** add to "sensitive data" a "financial account number, financial account log-in information or credit card or debit card number that, in combination with any required access or security code, password or credential, would allow access to a consumer's financial account"; the law now applies to anyone who processes sensitive data **with no volume threshold**; the GLBA entity exemption is narrowed to banks, credit unions, certain insurers and **registered broker-dealers**; minors get a ban on targeted ads and sale of data. **[S]** (Perkins Coie summary; statute text not read **[U]**)
- **California:** CCPA "sensitive personal information" includes "account log-in, financial account, debit card, or credit card number in combination with any required security or access code"; the CCPA applies to businesses over the adjusted revenue threshold ($25,000,000 as adjusted), or that buy, sell or share data of 100,000 or more consumers, or earn 50% or more of revenue from selling or sharing data. **[P]**
- **Virginia (VCDPA):** applicability thresholds and sensitive categories not re-read. **[U]**
- **Build rule:** never collect account numbers, credentials, tax returns or brokerage statements. Accredited status is a **single attestation** ("I am an accredited investor under Rule 501(a)") with a basis category, no figures. Net worth and income figures stay out of the database.
- Apple privacy label and Play data-safety form are derived from the shipped code per the agent rule, row by row, at the submitted commit.

## 9. Recommended safest v1 shape

**v1 is a verified professional network, not a deal platform.** Startups and Main Street businesses on one side, accredited equity investors on the other (lenders, revenue-based funders and community-round investors wait for a licensed partner, section 6A.4). Business owners and investors each create their own profile (no imported or scraped data), state objective facts (industry, stage, location; for investors, thesis and typical cheque size), and can search and filter each other on those facts. A connection only opens when **both** sides opt in, after which they can message in-app; everything that happens after that (decks, terms, money) happens off the platform between them and their own lawyers. The app shows **no raise amounts, valuations, instruments, terms, data rooms, commitments or investment flows**, never touches investment money, never scores or recommends a company as an investment, and takes **no fee that depends on any investment**. Revenue is a **flat, outcome-blind membership that includes matching** (in-app purchase on iOS and Android, Square on the web), the same for every subscriber in a tier, with introduction requests included rather than priced (section 6A.3). Investor accounts self-certify accredited status as a single attestation; identity is verified by a vendor that returns pass or fail; Elevare stores no IDs or financial records. The app is 18+, US-only, published by ELEVARESTUDIO LLC on an organisation developer account, listed under Business or Social Networking, with full UGC filtering, reporting, blocking, moderation and in-app account deletion.

**Three features that must NOT ship without a licence or a licensed partner:**
1. **Any compensation tied to investment:** success fees, percentage of raise, equity or carry, per-introduction or per-meeting fees, paid "boost" of a raise.
2. **Offering and transaction mechanics:** raise amounts, valuations or terms on profiles, deal rooms, term sheets, commitment buttons, Reg CF campaigns, or any handling, escrow or routing of investment money.
3. **Investment-merit recommendations:** scores, rankings, "top deals," AI opinions on whether to invest, or Elevare staff pitching specific companies to specific investors.

**Later, with a partner (each needs its own legal review):**
1. **Registered funding portal partner:** let businesses link from their profile to a live Reg CF campaign hosted by the portal; the portal runs the offering; Elevare is paid only a flat software or referral fee that the portal's Rule 402(b) safe harbour permits (referral pay "not tied to securities sales," and no investor personal information shared). **[P]**
2. **Registered broker-dealer partner for Reg D:** offering pages, 506(c) verification and deal flow run inside the broker-dealer's systems; Elevare licenses software or white-labels, without transaction-based pay to Elevare.
3. **Accreditation verification partner:** a broker-dealer, RIA, attorney or CPA issues method (C) letters; the investor carries a "verified accredited" credential the app displays but does not create.
4. **Registered or exempt investment adviser affiliate (AngelList model):** syndicates and SPVs with carry. This is a fund-management business with its own registration, custody and AML duties; not a feature.
5. **Rule 148-style demo days** run with a listed sponsor (university, accelerator, angel group), with Elevare charging only flat software or ticketing fees.
6. **Main Street lending and revenue-based funding:** either a licensed lender or registered commercial-financing broker partner runs the financing side, or Elevare itself registers as a commercial financing broker in Connecticut (NMLS) and a sales-based financing broker in Virginia (SCC) and clears California and every other launch state first.
7. **Community rounds:** link-out to a registered Reg CF portal or, for Virginia-only raises, to an intrastate offering run on the issuer's own site, a broker-dealer or a compliant portal under 21 VAC 5-40-190.

## 10. Open questions a securities lawyer must answer before launch

1. Is a **flat subscription** for a network on which businesses and investors meet "compensation in connection with the purchase or sale" of securities, or broker compensation under the multi-factor test, if Elevare never sees offering terms or money? Does it matter whether issuers, investors or both pay?
2. May a profile carry a neutral "open to investor conversations" signal, and if so, visible to whom, without causing **general solicitation** for 506(b) issuers or making Elevare a solicitor?
3. **New York:** does the Martin Act or GBL 359-e dealer registration reach a flat-fee introduction network? Any **Connecticut Department of Banking** or **Virginia SCC** position on online introduction networks?
4. May a business **link to its live Reg CF campaign** on a registered portal from its profile, and under what conditions?
5. Is "suggested matches" ordered by overlap of **user-stated objective fields** (shown with reasons) safe from **investment adviser** status federally and in CT and VA?
6. Does **section 230** protect Elevare against SEC civil or state securities enforcement over user posts, and how far can moderation go before Elevare "develops" content?
7. What **terms of use** clauses (no offering terms, bad-actor attestation, accredited self-certification, no-fee-arrangements) are enforceable and sufficient, and is arbitration with a class waiver advisable?
8. If the answer to the owner question is "everyday local investors," what is the minimum lawful structure (funding portal partnership only, or a Reg A route), and what would Elevare be allowed to be paid?

9. For **Main Street matching with lenders, merchant cash advance or revenue-based funders**: does a flat membership that includes matching make Elevare a "commercial financing broker" (Conn. Gen. Stat. 36a-861), a "sales-based financing broker" (Va. Code 6.2-2228) or a California Financing Law broker, and which other launch states have equivalent registration?
10. With **non-accredited Main Street community members** in the same app, what role and permission split (follow and buy only, no investment introductions) keeps the app from becoming a venue for unregistered public offerings, and is an accredited self-attestation gate enough for the investor role?
11. Is a **flat fee to request an introduction** (model (b) in section 6A) defensible in writing under the federal multi-factor test and the California and Texas finder definitions, or should it be ruled out permanently?

**Client (owner) confirmations needed:** whether investors will pay, businesses will pay, or both; that "charge to get the matching" will ship as a flat membership that includes matching, not a per-match price; that lenders, revenue-based funders and community rounds wait for a partner; US-only launch; 18+ only.
