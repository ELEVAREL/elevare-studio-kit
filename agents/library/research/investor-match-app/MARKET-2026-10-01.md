## 0. The blunt version (read this if nothing else)

1. **The owner has decided: both lanes, one app, paid matching.** This report plans for that. The evidence below does not change the decision. It changes the **order**, the **price design** and the **two conditions under which it fails**.
2. **Main Street businesses barely use investors.** In the Fed's 2025 Small Business Credit Survey, only **2% of small employer firms received an equity investment** in the prior 12 months. Of that 2%, **58% got it from friends or family, 23% from angels, 15% from VC, 4% from equity crowdfunding** [measured, Fed SBCS 2026 report p.25, N=6,355 and N=101]. What they want is **debt**: 60% applied for financing and 34% came up short [measured, p.12]. So in the Main Street lane, "investor" has to mean **lenders, CDFIs and local backers**, not just equity investors, or the lane matches people to a door almost nobody walks through.
3. **Pay to match is proven, but only one way round.** In dating, recruiting and startup investor tools, the side with urgency pays and the side with options is free: Tinder Gold $29.99/mo, LinkedIn Recruiter Lite $170 to $270/mo for 30 InMails, OpenVC $99/mo, Angel Match $59 to $349/mo [reported]. **Businesses pay, backers never pay.** Charge backers and there will be no backers.
4. **The failure mode is supply, not price.** A paid introduction that nobody accepts is a refund, a one-star review and a churned customer. The plan only works if a credit is spent **when a backer accepts**, and if no business is charged in a city until enough active backers exist there (section 2).
5. **Three legal walls shape the product** (the legal lane decides; section 6): a public feed showing raise terms is securities advertising (17 CFR 227.204) [measured]; "actively finding investors" for a specific raise is a broker indicator even with a flat fee [reported, law firm summaries]; and several states ban **advance fees** charged to borrowers for finding a loan [reported, state statutes]. So: no feed, match on profile fit not on a live raise, double opt-in introductions, and for lender matches the fee buys **software and verification**, never "a loan search".
6. **Recommendation:** one app, one profile and matching core, two entry lanes. **Main Street lane launches first** in one metro; the **startup lane opens in the same metro** once at least 15 active angels or funds are on the roster. Price: free profile, **$29/mo for 3 accepted introductions**, **$79/mo for 10**, extra introductions **$15 flat**. Start as a paid concierge with 20 businesses before writing code.

Market backdrop:
- Reg CF in 2025 raised between **$378M and almost $500M**; the two sources disagree. Offerings fell **28% to 29%**, the third straight year of decline [reported, Crowdfund Capital Advisors via Crowdfund Insider; second figure low confidence].
- Reg CF investors fell from **about 515,000 in 2021 to about 233,000 in 2025** [reported]. The pool of retail backers is shrinking.
- **36,207,130 small businesses** in the US [measured, SBA Office of Advocacy FAQ 2026].

### Who died, and why (inference from the facts above)
1. **Mainvest:** a % of a $50k to $150k raise is a small fee, while underwriting, compliance and escrow cost about the same at any size. Losses of about $2M a year were structural. Synapse only ended it.
2. **SeedInvest:** the parent's strategy changed. What sold was the investor email list, which tells you where the value in these platforms sits: **the backer roster**.
3. **Shapr and Bumble Bizz:** networking with no transaction and no deadline gives nobody a reason to come back tomorrow. Paid matching only survives if each match leads to a real next step.
4. **NextSeed:** too small to carry broker-dealer overhead alone, so it was absorbed.
5. **Gust (pivot, not death):** charging for founder to angel matching did not pay, so it became a paperwork company. That is the most direct warning for this plan, and the reason the plan below charges per *accepted* introduction instead of for access to a list.

## 2. One product, two lanes

### The two lanes, argued honestly
**Startup lane (founders seeking angels and VCs).**
- Demand: real, and founders already pay for intro volume (OpenVC, Angel Match, LinkedIn InMail).
- Harder side: investors, by far. They are buried in deal flow and pay nothing to get more. ACA member investment fell two years running [reported].
- Where Elevare wins: only locally. Elevare has no national investor network or founder brand. In one metro, a curated, verified, double opt-in roster can beat a cold list of 16,700 names.

**Main Street lane (salons, cafes, trades, boutiques seeking lenders, CDFIs and local backers).**
- Demand for money: large and measured. **34% of small employer firms had a financing shortfall and 15% had unmet needs**; of firms that borrowed online, **60% faced higher-than-expected costs** [measured, Fed SBCS].
- Demand for equity investors: small (2%) [measured].
- Harder side: local backers, but **lenders and CDFIs want borrowers**: 800+ lenders volunteer for SBA Lender Match [reported]. They will accept an introduction to a verified, packet-ready borrower because it is their pipeline.
- Where Elevare wins: this is the owner's existing audience, and he already builds their sites and runs their payments.

### How one product serves both without becoming two apps
| Layer | Shared or per lane | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Account, verification, profile | **Shared** | One profile object: who you are, verified owner identity, location, sector, stage, amount band, what kind of money you want (loan, revenue share, equity, grant, partner) |
| Packet and document vault | **Shared engine, per-lane templates** | Main Street template: lender-ready (statements, tax returns, licences). Startup template: deck, metrics, team |
| Backer profile | **Shared** | One backer object: type (lender, CDFI, angel, fund, community backer), geography, sectors, check or loan range, money types offered |
| Matching | **Shared engine, per-lane rules** | Fit score on profile fields, never on a live raise's terms. Same code, different weights |
| Introduction | **Shared** | Double opt-in. The business requests, the backer sees a profile summary, the backer accepts or passes, and only then are contact details exchanged. The platform then steps out |
| Entry lane | **Per lane** | First screen asks one question: "Are you a local business or a startup raising from investors?" The answer picks the questionnaire, template and backer pool. Everything after it is the same app |

What keeps it one app: one data model, one matching engine, one introduction flow, one price list. What genuinely differs: the questionnaire, the packet template and **the backer pool**. The two lanes share software, not supply. Recruiting backers is two separate jobs, and that is the real cost of "both".

### Which lane launches first, and why
**Main Street lane first, in one metro. Startup lane second, in the same metro, gated on supply.**
1. **The owner's credibility is with Main Street owners.** Elevare's first 20 paying businesses will come from that audience, not from founders.
2. **Main Street backers have a business reason to accept.** A lender or CDFI accepting an introduction is getting a pipeline. An angel accepting one is spending scarce attention.
3. **Free competition is weaker locally.** SBA Lender Match is free but stops at a list; nobody does verified, double opt-in, packet-ready local introductions.
4. **The startup lane opens when it can deliver.** Open it in the same metro once **at least 15 active angels, angel-group members or funds** have joined and accepted at least one introduction. Founders join a waitlist until then. A lane that charges before supply exists is the Gust failure.

### The cold start problem, and four ways to beat it
A paid match is worthless if no backer is there to accept it. Four solutions, ranked by fit for a one-founder studio:
1. **Recruit backers before charging any business (do this first).** Hand-recruit the first metro's roster: 10 to 20 lenders and CDFIs from the SBA Lender Match and CDFI Fund lists, the local angel group, and community backers. **Rule: no business in a category is charged until at least 5 active backers in that category and metro exist** [estimate].
2. **Single-player value for the business (do this).** The packet, readiness score and "which money fits you" list are worth something with zero matches. That keeps the free tier useful and gives the paid tier a floor.
3. **A curated founding cohort (do this).** 20 Main Street businesses, packets made partly by hand, paid from day one. Borrow Kiva's rule: each business can invite up to 40 of its own people as private supporters [measured, Kiva]. Those supporters are not matched to other businesses (that would be public solicitation); they are an extra reason for the business to stay.
4. **Piggyback on the Elevare Studio app (later).** The iOS app was only just submitted, so its paying base today is zero. It becomes a distribution channel once it has users. It cannot solve cold start this quarter.

### Supply the plan needs [estimate]
Assumptions: each paying business uses an average of 4 introduction requests a month, the target acceptance rate is 50%, and an active backer accepts about 4 introductions a month.
| Paying businesses | Requests per month | Accepted (50%) | Active backers needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | 400 | 200 | **50** |
| 1,000 | 4,000 | 2,000 | **500** |
| 10,000 | 40,000 | 20,000 | **5,000** |

The backer count, not the business count, is the number that limits growth. Track it first.

## 3. Who the backer side really is, and who will install an app

| Backer type | Lane | Funds the lane? | Will they install an app? | Why, and how to get them |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SBA lenders and CDFIs** | Main Street | Yes. The cheap money. 800+ on Lender Match [reported] | **Maybe, if it is their pipeline.** More likely they work from email notifications and a web view | They need verified, complete applicants. A loan officer accepting introductions is doing their job. Highest-value backer for lane 1 |
| **Local angels and angel groups** | Startup, rarely Main Street | Yes for startups; rarely for a cafe [reported, ACA] | **Yes, if deal quality is high.** They already use tools like AngelList | Curated, verified, local deal flow with no obligation. Recruit through the local angel group |
| **Small funds** | Startup | Yes for startups | Web, not app | Same as angels; fewer of them |
| **Community backers and regulars** | Main Street | Sometimes. Honeycomb has 20,000+ investors over 500+ businesses [measured] | **No.** They back a place they love, once | Best served by sending them to a registered portal (Honeycomb) for the actual raise. They are the business's own people, not a matching pool |
| **Friends and family** | Both | Yes. The biggest real source: 58% of equity recipients [measured] | **No.** They tap a link | Private supporter page, invite only. Never matched |
| **Revenue-based financing providers** | Both | Yes, as funders | API or email, not app | They would pay for leads, which looks like a referral fee. **Exclude in v1** |

**Conclusion:** the only backers who might open an app regularly are **lenders and CDFIs (lane 1) and active angels (lane 2)**. Build the backer side as a responsive web inbox with email and push notifications. The app store app is for businesses. "Social media" in the original framing should mean **introductions**, not a feed.

## 4. Monetisation: paying for matching

Hard constraint: **nothing contingent on money raised.** A flat fee for an introduction, charged whether or not any money ever moves, is the model. Two consequences, pending the legal lane:
- **A credit is spent when a backer accepts the introduction**, and refunded if the backer passes or does not answer in 14 days. That makes the fee contingent on a *conversation*, never on money. It also keeps the product honest: you pay for a real match, not a cold email.
- **For lender matches, the fee must buy software and verification, not "finding you a loan".** Several states ban advance fees charged to borrowers by loan brokers (Missouri, Iowa and Louisiana statutes surfaced in today's search) [reported]. The legal lane must confirm whether a subscription that includes lender introductions counts. If it does, Main Street lender introductions become free features of the subscription and the paid credits apply only to angels and community backers.

**What pay-to-match markets prove:**
1. The side with urgency pays; the side with options is free (founders and businesses pay; investors, lenders and candidates do not).
2. Subscriptions with a monthly **credit allowance** plus paid top-ups are the stable form (LinkedIn InMail, OpenVC daily intros, dating likes).
3. **Pay to pitch** (Keiretsu) is legal but founders resent it. Pay per *accepted* introduction avoids that.
4. Free lender matching (Lender Match) caps what Main Street will pay for lender introductions alone. The paid value has to be the packet, the verification and the acceptance.

### Who pays: recommendation
| Option | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Business only** | **Recommended for v1.** Matches every proven pay-to-match market. Backers join free, which is the only way to fill the roster |
| Backer only | No. Lenders paying per lead looks like a referral fee and possibly broker activity; angels will not pay for deal flow |
| Both | Not in v1. Revisit only a flat **backer workspace subscription** (for example $99/mo for lenders, CDFIs and angel groups: team seats, filters, pipeline export), never per lead or per deal, and only after the legal lane clears it and the roster is large enough that backers want tools |

### Pricing hypothesis [estimate]
One price list for both lanes, so it stays one app:
| Tier | Price | Includes |
|---|---|---|
| **Free** | $0 | Profile, readiness score, packet draft, "N backers fit you" count (the paywall moment, like dating apps' "see who likes you") |
| **Match** | $29/mo | 3 accepted introductions a month, finished packet, document vault, private supporter page |
| **Match Pro** | $79/mo | 10 accepted introductions a month, data room with view tracking, verified badge included, monthly update emails to backers |
| **Extra introduction** | $15 flat | One more accepted introduction. Refunded if not accepted in 14 days |
| **Verified badge** | $49 one-time (included in Pro) | Owner identity, EIN or state registration, read-only revenue connection |

Notes:
- If this ships inside the Elevare Studio app, map Match to the existing $29.99 tier and Match Pro to $79.99, so matching becomes the upgrade reason instead of a fourth price.
- The startup lane could likely bear higher prices (OpenVC $99, Angel Match up to $349). Keep one list at launch; test a startup-only price after 90 days.
- $15 per accepted introduction sits above LinkedIn's InMail (about $4.67 to $10) because an accepted, verified, double opt-in introduction is worth more than a message that may be ignored.

### The maths [estimate]
Assumptions, each stated:
- Mix: 65% Match at $29, 35% Match Pro at $79. Subscription ARPU = 0.65 x 29 + 0.35 x 79 = 18.85 + 27.65 = **$46.50**.
- Top-ups: 20% of payers buy 2 extra introductions a month at $15. Average = 0.20 x 30 = **$6.00**.
- Total ARPU = **$52.50/mo**.
- App store fee 15% (Apple Small Business Program rate, assuming in-app purchase). Infra, AI, verification and support 10%. Contribution = 52.50 x 0.75 = **$39.38/mo**.
- Verification fees left out of totals to stay conservative.

| Paying businesses | MRR | Gross ARR | Net after 15% store fee | Active backers needed (section 2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | $5,250 | $63,000 | $53,550 | 50 |
| 1,000 | $52,500 | $630,000 | $535,500 | 500 |
| 10,000 | $525,000 | $6,300,000 | $5,355,000 | 5,000 |

**Unit economics, base and downside [estimate]:**
| | Base | Downside |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly churn | 8% (capital need is episodic; people leave after they raise) | 15% |
| Average life | 12.5 months | 6.7 months |
| LTV (contribution) | 39.38 / 0.08 = **$492** | 39.38 / 0.15 = **$263** |
| CAC via in-app cross-sell | about $25 | about $25 |
| CAC via local outreach or paid | about $200 | about $200 |
| LTV:CAC at $200 CAC | **2.5 : 1** | **1.3 : 1** (fails) |
| CAC payback at $200 CAC | 200 / 39.38 = **5.1 months** | 5.1 months, against a 6.7 month average life |

**Most sensitive number: the acceptance rate, which then drives churn.** If backers accept under about 30% of requests, businesses burn through refunds, feel nothing happening, and churn moves toward the downside case. Measure acceptance rate in week one of the cohort, before anything else.

**Reachability:** 100 payers is a realistic first-year test for a one-founder studio. 1,000 needs the Elevare app to have real distribution and about 500 active backers. 10,000 needs a channel, a backer recruiting team and support staff that do not exist today. Treat that row as a ceiling, not a plan.

## 5. The smallest TRUE version

**The one thing it must do well on day one:** a business fills in one profile, gets a credible packet, and within 7 days **at least one verified backer who fits says yes to a conversation.** The accepted introduction is the product. Everything else supports it.

**Not in the smallest true version:** a public feed, swiping, backers browsing a marketplace, raise terms, money moving between parties, promoted listings, backer fees.

### First 90 days
1. **Days 1 to 14: legal gate, backer recruiting, 20 interviews.** Get the legal lane's verdict on section 6. Hand-recruit the first metro's backers: lenders and CDFIs from Lender Match and the CDFI Fund list, plus the local angel group. Interview 20 Main Street owners: "In the next six months, will you need money you do not have? How much, and for what?"
2. **Days 15 to 45: concierge matching, no code.** For up to 10 owners who said yes: build the packet by hand, pick 3 fitting backers by hand, and email each backer a double opt-in introduction. Charge $29 for 3 accepted introductions. Log every request, acceptance and outcome in a spreadsheet.
3. **Days 46 to 70: build only what the concierge proved.** If the numbers below hold, build the shared profile, packet, matching and introduction flow (Main Street lane only) as a tab in the Elevare Studio app or a minimal standalone app. The backer side is a web inbox.
4. **Days 71 to 90: founding cohort of 20 businesses in one metro.** Open the startup lane waitlist in the same metro and start recruiting angels for it. Open the lane only when at least 15 angels or funds are active.

### The three numbers that say continue or stop
1. **Backer acceptance rate:** at least **50%** of introduction requests accepted within 14 days. **Stop or fix supply if under 30%.**
2. **Willingness to pay:** at least **5 of the first 20** owners pay for introductions. **Stop if fewer than 2.**
3. **Life-changing outcome:** at least **3 of the first 20** get a yes (approved loan, CDFI funding, angel cheque, community raise launched on a registered portal) within 120 days. **Stop if zero.** The owner's own test is "this could change businesses lives"; if nobody gets money, it has not.

Guard rail: more than **5 owner hours a week** before number 3 is hit means pause. The Elevare Studio app launch comes first.

## 6. Three sharp risks, and what would have to be true to proceed

1. **Regulatory: paid matching reads as broker activity or advance-fee loan brokering.** Broker indicators go beyond % fees and include "actively finding investors", pre-screening investors and locating issuers for investors [reported, law firm summary of SEC positions]. Several states ban advance fees from borrowers to loan brokers [reported, state statutes]. Public raise terms break Reg CF Rule 204 and 506(b) [measured, eCFR 227.204]. Apple guideline 3.2.1(viii) expects investing apps to come from the institution performing the service [reported].
   **To proceed:** the legal lane confirms in writing that this design needs no registration or licence: (i) fees are flat, refunded only for non-acceptance, never tied to money; (ii) matching is on profile fit, never on a live offering's terms; (iii) the platform makes a double opt-in introduction and steps out, with no part in negotiation; (iv) no raise terms appear anywhere in the app; (v) lender introductions are priced in a way that is not an advance fee in the launch state. If (v) fails, lender introductions become free inside the subscription.
2. **Supply: backers do not show up or do not accept, so paid matches go unanswered.** Investors are oversupplied with deals; ACA member investment is falling [reported]; retail Reg CF investors fell 55% since 2021 [reported]. A paid introduction nobody accepts is worse than no product.
   **To proceed:** 50 active backers recruited in the launch metro **before** the cohort pays, and a measured acceptance rate of at least 50% in the concierge phase. The startup lane stays closed until 15 active angels or funds exist.
3. **Owner attention and episodic churn.** Two lanes means two backer recruiting jobs, during the first month of the first app. Capital need is one-off, so churn may sit near the downside case (LTV:CAC 1.3).
   **To proceed:** Main Street lane only for the first 90 days, capped at 5 owner hours a week, with no new standalone codebase until the three numbers hold. Continue past day 90 only if cohort churn is at or under 8% a month or matching measurably lifts upgrades to the $29.99 and $79.99 tiers. **What to stop to make room:** no startup-lane build before supply exists, no separate brand before the numbers hold, no backer-side product beyond a web inbox.

## 7. Positioning and names

**Who it is for:**
- Main Street lane: the owner of a salon, cafe, trade business or boutique who needs $10k to $250k in the next six months and does not know who to ask.
- Startup lane: a local founder raising a first round who has no warm path to the angels in their own city.

**The specific pain:** they do not know which money fits, they cannot get a warm introduction, and cold asks get ignored. Online lenders cost more than expected (60% of online borrowers said so [measured]).
**Why us over the alternatives:** crowdfunding portals take 6% to 8% of the raise; we take nothing from the money. OpenVC and LinkedIn give you names to cold-email; we give you verified backers who said yes to talking. SBA Lender Match gives you a list; we give you a packet and a conversation. Doing nothing means owner funds and credit cards.

### Three one-sentence positionings, seeded from "this could change businesses lives"
1. **"The right backer can change a business's life: we find the ones who fit you, and you only pay when they say yes to meeting you."**
2. **"From the corner shop to the next big startup, get introduced to the lenders and investors who can fund your next chapter, and keep every dollar you raise."**
3. **"Your business is one introduction away from its next chapter: verified backers, real conversations, and never a cut of what you raise."**

Recommended: **1**. It carries his mission line, names the pay-to-match mechanic honestly, and the "only pay when they say yes" promise is the trust hook the pay-to-pitch players never had. All three promise introductions, not funding; keep it that way (claim to reality parity), and run the final line past the legal lane.

### Own brand or a feature inside the Elevare Studio app?
**For a feature inside the app:**
- Owner attention is the binding constraint: one codebase, one App Store listing, one support inbox.
- The Main Street lane serves the same people the app already serves, and matching becomes the reason to upgrade to $29.99 and $79.99.
- Zero new marketing cost; it compounds with the client base.
- Caveat: do not add it before iOS 1.0 is approved. An investing-sounding feature in a first review risks the core app.

**For its own brand (stronger now that startups are in scope):**
- Startup founders and angels will not adopt a "business assistant for salons" app. The startup lane needs a neutral brand.
- Backers need a trust-first identity; a loan officer or angel should not have to install a salon owner's assistant app.
- It keeps regulatory exposure walled off from the main app's App Store standing.
- A standalone brand with two lanes is a clearer story for the owner's mission line.

**Verdict:** run the 90 days as a **Main Street feature inside the Elevare Studio app** (or a concierge with no app at all). **Spin out to its own brand (Rightbacker) when the startup lane opens**, which happens only once 15 angels are active and the three numbers hold. The startup lane is the event that makes a separate brand worth its cost.

## 8. Recommendation

Build the owner's "both" as **one app with a shared profile, matching and introduction core and two entry lanes**. **Launch the Main Street lane first** in one metro, with backers recruited before any business pays. **Open the startup lane in the same metro** once 15 angels or funds are active. **Businesses pay, backers are free.** Charge **$29/mo for 3 accepted introductions, $79/mo for 10, $15 per extra**, with credits refunded when a backer does not accept and nothing ever tied to money raised. Test it as a hand-run concierge for 20 businesses before writing code. Gate everything on the legal lane's verdict in section 6 and on iOS 1.0 approval. Cap it at 5 owner hours a week.

**The one question for the owner:** which of the five active clients (ProCraft VA, Petal & Threads, Coffee Baby, Fanta, PhyscoClosett) has asked about raising or borrowing money in the last year, how much, and for what? The answer picks the launch metro and the first cohort member, and tells us whether the Main Street lane has a real first customer. If none has, that is the first data point.
