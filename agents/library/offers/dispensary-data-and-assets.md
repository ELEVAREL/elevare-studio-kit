## 0. Naming caution, read before writing any client-facing copy

**Do not ship the word "audit" until compliance clears it.** In several states "audit" is a restricted term of art reserved for licensed CPAs, and using it for a marketing engagement is an avoidable risk in an already over-regulated industry.

- **Internal shorthand:** the audit.
- **Client-facing default, used everywhere in this document's copy:** **Dispensary Performance Review**.
- **Approved alternates, swappable with no structural change:** Retail Performance Assessment, Storefront and Listing Assessment, Merchandising Review.
- **Never pair the deliverable name with** "financial", "accounting", "attest", "assurance", or "examination", in any state, regardless of the clearance outcome.

The offer is built so the name is a single swappable token. Changing it is a find-and-replace in the sell sheet and proposal template, nothing more.

## 1. What Elevare is in this vertical

**Elevare is a marketing production company that also does performance reviews.** Two hats, both ordinary professional services:

1. **Outside reviewer.** We assess what is publicly observable about a retailer's storefront, menu, listings and published marketing, score it against a printed rubric, and flag compliance exposure.
2. **Production vendor.** We build finished creative files. The retailer publishes them.

We are **not** their marketing department, **not** the advertiser of record, and **emphatically not a data company**. We do not run campaigns, buy media, manage ad accounts, or post on their behalf. We do not hold consumer data as a product, build a data set from it, sell it, or benchmark one client against another.

One sentence repeats on every client-facing surface: **we produce the files, you publish them.**

## 2. The competitive seam, and why v2 was pointed at the wrong one

**springbig and Alpine IQ already own the data → insight → customer-action loop.** They own it as software, on a POS integration the dispensary has already approved, at $500 to $2,500 a month. A retailer running either one has already bought "your data tells you what to do next." **Elevare cannot win that head-on and will not try.**

**The defensible seam is not "data plus assets." It is "assets, informed by data," where the data half is table stakes rather than the product.**

What that means concretely:

They do not make the shelf talker, the menu board slide, the case card, the window poster, the motion piece, or a compliant Massachusetts warning lockup that does not look bolted on. Their template libraries are stock, and stock is why so much cannabis retail creative looks the same.

That is a qualified prospect who has pre-agreed to our premise and found the gap themselves.
- **Never sell against them. Never propose replacing them.** The line is: *"Keep springbig. It will tell you who to talk to and when. It will not design what you say. That is us."* A studio that positions as a springbig competitor loses on integration depth and deserves to.
- **Referral or partnership channel is worth researching**, since we are complementary rather than competitive. **Flag only — do not represent any relationship that does not exist.**

**Consequence for the product architecture:** the POS-data work stops being the headline and becomes a confirmatory add-on sold to a client who already trusts us. Everything below follows from that.

## 4. §280E, corrected

Under IRC §280E a state-legal cannabis business cannot deduct ordinary business expenses federally, and marketing and professional services sit squarely in the non-deductible bucket. §280E does not block reducing gross receipts by cost of goods sold under §471.

**The v2 correction.** v2 priced against a **+26.6%** premium. That figure implicitly assumed no state-level relief. **Both primary markets decouple:**

- **Massachusetts — St. 2022 c.180 §§1-2.** Cannabis businesses may take state deductions disallowed federally by §280E. MA corporate excise is 8.0%.
- **Connecticut — Conn. Gen. Stat. § 12-217(a)(1)(H).** Same effect at the state level. CT corporate is 7.5%.

**So the penalty is the lost federal 21% only: about $210 extra per $1,000 billed, not $266.**

*Arithmetic footnote for whoever presents this, so nobody re-litigates it: $210 and $266 are the same 21% measured against different bases. $210 is 21% of the invoice. $266 is the gross-up relative to what a fully deductible $1,000 would net-cost a normal business ($790). **We adopt the invoice basis — multiply by 1.21 — because it is the conservative framing and the one a buyer can check on their own invoice.** The substantive correction is the state decoupling, which is real and worth roughly 8 points in MA.*

| $795 one-time | ~$962 |
| $595 / mo | ~$720 |
| $1,150 one-time | ~$1,392 |

**The threshold rule that follows:** to keep a monthly under $2,000 of perceived cost, the sticker ceiling is **$1,650**. To keep a one-time under $1,000 perceived, the sticker ceiling is **$826**. Both drive the prices below.

**Kept from v2, unchanged:**
1. **Argue in gross margin, never in "saved spend."** An improvement that flows through COGS actually reduces taxable income; an expense reduction does not help nearly as much. Lead with it.
2. **The COGS question is flagged, never answered.** Elevare **itemizes every invoice by deliverable** rather than a lump "marketing services" line, so the client's CPA has something to work with, and raises the question exactly once: *"Ask your cannabis CPA whether any part of this touches inventory-related cost. We will not tell you the answer and we will not put a characterization in writing."* **Internal caution stands: aggressive §471(c) positions have drawn real IRS scrutiny. Never coach one. This is objection handling, never a benefit, and it never appears in the sell sheet.**

### 7.1 Compliance exposure: the hook, and the discipline that makes it safe

A compliance finding is the single most attention-getting thing you can put in front of a licensee, and Massachusetts independents get this wrong constantly. **935 CMR 500.105(4) requires "Please Consume Responsibly" plus at least two prescribed warnings, in their entirety, conspicuously on the face of any advertisement created for public viewing.** Most independent social posts and listing banners carry neither. We also check the 85% audience-evidence posture, brand-name restrictions (no medical symbols, no product or paraphernalia imagery appealing to under-21s, no colloquial cannabis references), and anything deceptive, misleading or ambiguous.

**Because being wrong here would be worse than saying nothing, the discipline is absolute:**

- **We never write "you are in violation."** The finding format is fixed: *"This post does not appear to carry the warning statements that 935 CMR 500.105(4) requires on the face of public-facing advertising."* Then the regulation text quoted, then a dated screenshot, then: *"This is an observation, not legal advice. Your compliance counsel should confirm."*
- **Every finding cites the regulation and shows the evidence.** No finding ships without both.
- **No finding is ever generalized into a claim about the operator**, only about the specific artifact observed on a specific date.
- **The cold email leads with the offer, never the verdict.** We do not open a first contact with an unverified accusation about a stranger's licence. We say what the Review looks at. They ask.

>

### 9.1 Data intake: how Elevare never possesses identifiable consumer data
*Kept from v2 in full. Applies only to this add-on, which is now the only part of the offer that touches client data at all.*

**The allowlist is the control and it runs first.** Any column not on the allowlist causes the whole file to be **rejected and deleted**, with a machine-readable rejection log naming the offending column returned to the client. A denylist would miss the next field name.

**Accepted, the whole list:** `transaction_id` (pseudonymous per-transaction key, never linked to a person) · `transaction_datetime` · `location_id` · `line_number` · `sku` · `product_name` · `brand` · `category` · `subcategory` · `strain_name` · `strain_type` · `size_or_weight` · `unit_of_measure` · `quantity` · `unit_price` · `discount_amount` · `line_total` · `tax_amount` · `unit_cost` (optional) · `on_hand_quantity` (optional) · `register_id` (optional) · `sale_channel` · `transaction_type` (a flag on the transaction, never on a person) · `employee_code` (opaque code, never a name).

**Rejected on sight:** customer name, customer ID, loyalty ID, phone, email, address, date of birth, age, government or driver's licence number, medical patient or caregiver registration number, remaining purchase allotment, **any hashed or tokenized customer identifier**, any free-text note field. Hashed IDs are rejected specifically: a salted hash is still pseudonymous personal data, and accepting it would make Elevare a processor of consumer data.

**Repeat purchase without touching a customer.** Computed at the product level: units per day per SKU, days-of-supply, time-to-restock, shelf repeat-turn. If a client wants person-level cohorts they run it **inside their own system** from our template and return only the aggregate. Elevare never receives the rows, not even hashed.

**Isolation, per the studio's absolute rule.** One isolated store per client, per-client credential, per-client bucket, per-client compute. No shared inbox, no shared drop folder, never email attachments. **No cross-client table exists.** Logs record a file ID, row count and validation result, never a row. Every job asserts the tenant before reading a byte. Raw exports retained 13 months for year-over-year, deleted within 30 days of termination with the date given to the client.

**Benchmarking is a separate product that does not exist.** Client-facing: *"We do not compare you to other retailers and we will never show your numbers to anyone. If we ever build a benchmark product it will be a separate agreement you sign separately, it will require a minimum number of participating stores before any figure is produced, and no individual store will be identifiable in it."* Internally: separate written permission per client and a k-anonymity floor of at least 5 stores per reported cell. Do not structure storage to make it convenient; convenience is the failure mode.

**Two-sided control.** The client signs a one-page data scope attestation; our validator enforces the same thing independently. Neither side relies on the other's care.

## 10. Creative constraints written into production

**Never, in any asset, in any state, even on request:** health, medical, therapeutic, wellness, dosing or symptom-relief claims. "Safe", "clean", "organic", "natural" unless independently certified with the certificate on file. Youth appeal of any kind. Anything deceptive, misleading or ambiguous.

**Massachusetts (primary).** 85% 21+ audience on reliable, current composition data. **"Please Consume Responsibly" plus at least two prescribed warnings in their entirety, conspicuously on the face of any public-facing advertisement.** Brand names may not use medical symbols, marijuana or paraphernalia imagery appealing to under-21s, or colloquial cannabis references.

> **The warning lockup is a layout constraint, not a footnote, and it is our craft advantage.** *(Kept from v2, and now doubly load-bearing since it is also the compliance hook.)* Every MA public-facing template ships with a compliant warning lockup **designed into the grid from the first pass**, sized to stay legible at delivered dimensions. Retrofitting a warning block onto a finished design is why agency cannabis creative looks bad. Designing around it from the start is a genuine, demonstrable, sellable difference.

**Connecticut (secondary, strictest).** 90% audience. **No image or visual representation of the cannabis plant or any part of it** in advertising, which is why typographic and graphic craft rather than photography is the product there. Exterior signage limited to a single unilluminated 16x18in sign, no cannabis brand names or graphics on the exterior, product not clearly visible from outside. *Cited from the dispensary-facility marketing regulation; adult-use provisions sit in P.A. 21-1 §33 and the DCP adult-use regulations. Confirm the current citation with counsel before it appears in an agreement.* Placement restrictions and billboard time-of-day rules make outdoor essentially unusable. **We do not produce discount or price-promotion creative for a CT client without their compliance officer's written approval of the specific mechanic, and we never propose one unprompted.**

**Rhode Island.** Looser than CT, closer to MA. Confirm per engagement; MA templates do not transfer without a rules check.

**Meta and Google both prohibit paid cannabis advertising**, so everything targets owned surfaces: in-store, menu screens, website and menu, email and SMS, Weedmaps and Leafly, organic social.

**The chemistry line, drawn explicitly.** The COA module tells the *buyer* that limonene-forward flower outsells in their evening slot. It does not put "limonene lifts your mood" on a shelf talker. Chemistry may be stated factually (compound names, percentages, batch, lab). Effects may not be stated at all.

**Production standard.** Assets are designed through the studio's HTML build system rendered to PNG and press-ready PDF: real typography, the client's real tokens pulled from their live site source, layered composition, proper hierarchy. No stock-gradient slop, no emoji, no AI-generated imagery unless the client specifically asks and approves. Photo slots left for the client's own product photography where permitted. Every delivery ships as one self-contained HTML gallery with each asset embedded, opening on any machine with no folders and no broken links.

### A.1 The disqualification gate: an equity flag does not mean independent

**Before treating any Economic Empowerment, social-equity or DBE-flagged licensee as a prospect, open the CCC filing and read the persons and entities having direct or indirect control.**

**Worked example, and the reason this gate exists.** Lemonnade Springfield carries an EE flag and a 51% local majority owner. **Cookies Retail LLC, Cookies Holdings LLC and three TRP entities hold direct or indirect control of the licence.** The flag is accurate as filed and tells you almost nothing about who decides.

Two reasons this is a hard gate rather than a nicety:

1. **Accuracy.** Pitching an MSO subsidiary as an under-resourced independent is the kind of error a recipient forwards to their group, and it costs the sending domain more than the one prospect.
2. **Commercial.** A brand-licensed store **already has a brand system imposed by its licensor**. There is no identity to build, no template system to own, and no authority at store level to approve creative. There is nothing here to sell, so the check pays for itself before it protects anything.

**What to read:** the licence filing's direct-and-indirect-control disclosure, plus any management services agreement or IP licensing agreement on file. **If a multi-state operator or a national brand licensor appears anywhere in it, the licensee is not an independent regardless of the flag.**

## D. SEGMENT C — The inverted motion, where data leads and assets follow

### D.1 Why the v3 pitch dies on contact

The worked prospect: Terpene Journey, Swampscott MA. Custom Webflow site, terpene-first taxonomy (shop by 8 terpene profiles, shop by activity), in-house menu carrying TAC, THC, CBD and terpene percentages per SKU, a recommendation tool, a loyalty program, an education library, 10 town-specific landing pages. **They scored 5/10 on the v3 thesis and that score is wrong** — it measured the size of the gap we normally fill, and this store has already filled it, better than most agencies would.

**You cannot sell a diagnostic to someone who diagnoses better than you.** The v3 Review's findings for this store would be thin, obvious, or wrong, and delivering it would cost the relationship permanently. **Segment C never sees a Review.** Not a discounted one, not a shortened one.

### D.2 What they actually lack

One co-founder is a data scientist and the entire store is built on terpene taxonomy. That is the most receptive audience imaginable for recurring consumer-behavior work, but the pitch is not "here is what is selling," because they know.

The pitch is the question their positioning demands and their tools cannot answer:

> **Does your terpene taxonomy actually predict purchase behaviour in your own store, or does price?**

Their loyalty platform tells them who to text. Their menu publishes chemistry. **Nothing they own joins the two and tests whether the thesis the whole store is built on holds up in their own baskets.** That is a real, unmet, intellectually interesting question, and it is worth money to exactly this buyer.

Three things they lack, in order:
1. **Time.** A data-scientist co-founder running a retail store is doing analysis at midnight or not at all.
2. **Basket-level reads that are tedious rather than hard** — affinity and lift by terpene profile, price-band performance within chemistry cohorts, cross-elasticity between profiles.
3. **A standing, external, methodologically stated answer** to whether the taxonomy earns its place.

### D.3 The product

**The Chemistry Read.** v3's Sales Read plus the COA module, sold **standalone, at a higher price, with no Review first.**

**Comp check:** Happy Cabbage is ~$900/mo for standalone analytics with no chemistry linkage and no design attached. $895/mo sits exactly on that line while answering a question they do not, which is the correct place to be.

### D.4 The two things that make this credible to this buyer

**1. Ship a methods appendix.** State the affinity math (support, confidence, lift), the minimum support threshold, how multi-item baskets and ties are handled, how terpene percentages are normalized across batches (batch-weighted, not naive per-SKU averaging), and how sparse cells are treated. **This costs us almost nothing and it is the entire difference between a report a data scientist trusts and one they audit for errors in front of us.**

**2. Do not oversell certainty to a statistician.** A single store's basket data is small-n for many of the questions worth asking. **Mark thin cells, state confidence honestly, and write "inconclusive" where it is** — which is house standard anyway (STANDARDS, "INCONCLUSIVE is a first-class result"). With this buyer specifically, an honest null finding builds more credibility than a confident one they can pick apart.

### D.5 The opener, and what never to say

**Lead with the question, never with a critique of their surfaces.** Anything resembling "your online presence could be stronger" is factually wrong here and reads as a template.

> *"Your whole store is organised around terpene profiles. Do your own baskets agree? We read twelve months of your sales against your COAs and tell you which profiles actually drive repeat purchase and which are just well-merchandised. Methods appendix included, so you can check our work."*

**Never offer them:** a Performance Review, listing setup, "we'll fix your menu descriptions", or a surface score. Every one of those insults a store that has already done the work better than the pitch assumes.
